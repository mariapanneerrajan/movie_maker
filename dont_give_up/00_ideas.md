# 00 — Ideas

## Set 1

Seed: the essence of Isaac at Rehoboth — prosperity draws envy, repeated dispossession,
repeated rebuilding, and finally room without strife. Feeling to transfer: encouragement
to get back up and rebuild, ending in earned, uncontested belonging.

1. Cut loose twice from the warm harbours he lights in the void, a lantern-keeper anchors his
   last lamp to a wild comet — and its glow opens a harbour wide enough for the ships that cast
   him out.
   Anchor: Genesis 26:22, "he has made room for us," plus Abimelech coming to Isaac for peace;
   the rivals arrive not to seize but to shelter. Internal only — carried as light in the black.
   Why it works: two silent cuts of a tether, then a comet blooming into a harbour a fleet
   tethers to; the turn is his hand waving them in — vindicated welcome.

2. Twice driven off the buried water-lines he digs, a desert tunneller bores beneath the one dune
   nobody claims — and a green river surfaces, racing across the sand faster than his rivals can
   plant flags.
   Anchor: Genesis 26 entire — Esek, Sitnah, Rehoboth — with 26:12, sowing in famine and reaping
   a hundredfold. Internal only — the shaft-line is the well dug three times.
   Why it works: overhead vertical of shaft-craters going dark under shovelled dust, twice, then
   a green thread tearing across orange dunes; the turn is pure landscape — exhilaration that the
   work was never wasted.

3. Twice stripped of the fog-catching rig that waters his block, a rooftop gardener climbs to his
   father's derelict tower and unfurls a sail so wide that rain falls on every roof beneath him.
   Anchor: Genesis 26:18, Isaac re-digging his father's stopped-up wells, joined to the meekness
   that inherits the earth (Matthew 5:5). Internal only — he never fights back, he only re-ties
   the knot.
   Why it works: the same hand-tied knot three times, the last on his father's rusted frame;
   the turn is water spilling onto the roofs that took his rig — tenderness toward the takers.

4. A sky-nomad who cuts holes in the cloud-floor to rain on the dead world below is sealed out
   twice, until he opens the wild thunderhead no clan will stand on and green climbs up to meet
   him.
   Anchor: Genesis 26's yield-and-re-dig pattern against rain given to deserving and undeserving
   alike (Matthew 5:45) and streams in the desert (Isaiah 35). Internal only — the cloud is the
   well.
   Why it works: the strangest of the five — kneeling on cloud, cutting a shaft, rain falling
   away beneath his feet; the turn is a standing river of rain and the clans drifting over to
   stand in his weather.

5. Twice fenced out of the lamp-gardens he plants on the dark seabed, a diver seeds the back of a
   sleeping leviathan — which rises and blooms an entire trench in its wake.
   Anchor: Genesis 26's third, uncontested well, carried by the grain of wheat that falls into
   the ground before it bears much fruit (John 12:24). Internal only.
   Why it works: a warm picture-book monster carrying a garden of coral-lamps through black
   water, seeds falling like sparks; the turn is that his most hopeless planting is the one
   nobody can fence — awe with relief inside it.

Recommendation: #2, the desert tunneller — the climax is the theme, it needs no invented
physics to read in 30 seconds on a phone, and the shaft-line gives Act Two three clean beats.
#4 is the wild card if a stranger film is wanted.
