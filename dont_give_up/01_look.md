# LOOK — THE THIRD SHAFT

**Logline:** Twice driven off the buried water-lines he digs, a desert tunneller bores beneath the
one dune nobody claims — and a green river surfaces, racing across the sand faster than his rivals
can plant flags.

**Anchor:** *(the pattern only — it is never named, quoted, cited or labelled anywhere, including
here)* A man who prospers is envied; what he opens is stopped up and taken from him, twice, and
each time he moves on rather than fights. The third time he digs where nobody else wants the
ground, and there nobody contends with him — room is made for him, and what he opens returns many
times over. **The symbol carrying it is the bore-hole dug three times** — a hole in dead ground
that gives water, taken twice, and given a third time on ground nobody wanted, where the river runs
out across open land and no one comes to claim it. No verse, citation, book name, or personal name
from the source appears in this file or in any downstream deliverable; the banned surface words
*well*, *Isaac*, *Rehoboth*, *God* and *bless* appear nowhere in the film or its prompts.

**Feeling to leave behind:** The work you were driven off was never wasted — keep digging, and the
ground that finally opens for you will be larger than everything they took, and no one will come
for it.

## Format
**16:9 horizontal, this film only** — a deliberate per-film exception. Scale reads across the width:
the ridge of riders runs the full frame width, the dust and water plumes fall across the whole
desert, the closing river crosses the frame edge to edge. Rajan is placed small and off-centre in
the wides so the land has room to be enormous around him.

## The two bursts — the film's bookend spectacle, and its highest-priority images
Shot 1's sand eruption and shot 6's water eruption are the same frame twice, and they carry the
whole arc. Both are full-scale blockbuster set-pieces, not effects garnish:
- **Identical geometry.** Straight down, hole dead centre, column firing up into the lens, plume
  mushrooming and falling back across the full width. Nothing else about the framing changes.
- **Sand burst (0:00–0:03):** dry, brown-grey, ten storeys, boiling as it climbs, a shockwave ring
  skating out over four dune crests, grit sheeting the lens. It falls as a slow brown rain and
  buries everything in dust before Rajan is revealed inside it.
- **Water burst (0:27–0:29):** taller than the sand went, white and jade at its heart, sunlit
  through, rainbows in its own spray, falling back as shining rain across the same width. Where the
  sand fell dead and dry, the water falls bright and loud.
- **The last note of the film is the rain landing on Rajan, and his joy.** From the top-down his joy
  must read through the whole body, not the face: soaked through, head tipped back, arms wide, a
  slow turn in place under falling water. That turn is the closing performance — hold it, do not cut
  it short for more landscape. Shot 5's close-up carries the same joy on the face one beat earlier:
  open, streaming, lifted.
- **The closing frames are empty of rivals.** No flag, banner, claim pole, snapped pole, rider or
  strider appears anywhere in shot 6, at any distance, including the horizon line. Rajan, the
  column, and the water are the only things in the last frame.
- **Shot 5 sets it up in close:** the crust splits and the water breaks past his face — the same
  eruption seen from inside it, one beat before shot 6 shows its full size. Shot 5 is equally clear
  of any rival element.

## Colour script
- **Act One (0:00–0:06):** sun-bleached orange and ochre dune field under a hard white midday sun.
  The eruption adds one brown-grey mass. Two accents only — the jade-green thread of water, and
  Rajan's indigo work-coat.
- **Act Two (0:06–0:24):** narrows and cools in two stages. Shot 2 holds Act One's orange but drains
  the green out of frame. From shot 3 (0:11) the palette drops to dusty rose and grey-violet, the
  new water's jade the last saturated thing left — and it is buried on screen. From shot 4 (0:18)
  colour is gone: bleached blue-grey moonlight, black sand, white wind. Saturation bottoms at 0:24.
  Crimson lives only on the rival banners, and it leaves the film for good at 0:18.
- **Act Three (0:24–0:30):** green-gold dawn arriving from below the frame line at shot 5 (0:24);
  shot 6 (0:27) restores Act One's exact orange dune field, now cut through by jade-green — **and
  every green in this film is water, never plant life.** The green in frame is the colour of the
  river and the light coming off it; the sand it crosses stays orange and bare.
- The single node's continuity block states all three act-palette states and names the switch shots:
  **warm orange → cool violet at shot 3 (0:11); cool violet → monochrome blue-grey at shot 4
  (0:18); monochrome → green-gold warmth at shot 5 (0:24); Act One orange restored, plus jade
  river, at shot 6 (0:27).** Never a film-wide average palette.

## Grade and render character
Large-format desert-epic photochemical: fine grain, deep blacks that stay blue rather than crushing,
highlights allowed to blow out softly on wet sand and water spray. Texture lives in sand grain,
canvas weave and brass patina — weathered, never grimy. No digital over-sharpening; sky gradients
stay smooth.

## Lighting design
**Through Acts One and Two, light always comes from above and behind him** — sun, sky, the ridge the
riders own. Something is always over him. **In Act Three light arrives from below the frame line,
out of the ground**, green-gold, and it is the first light in the film he is not standing under.
Grace comes up, not down. Flag-riders are backlit silhouettes with warm rim light — never underlit,
never shadowed as villains, never lit to read as cruel.

## Camera rule
**The camera never retreats from Rajan.** Every move is toward him or straight down onto him. One
single-intent move per shot: shot 1 static top-down; 2 slow push down the slope toward him; 3 push
in, holding him as he goes down and rides back up into frame with him; 4 push in on the rig; 5 slow
push to close; 6 static top-down (locks the rhyme). The only camera impact in the film is a single
small jolt on the push in shot 3.

## Lens language
- Shots 1 and 6 — **wide 84° top-down**, identical geometry both times: the desert fills the width,
  the plume grows into the lens. This is the rhyme, and the two frames must match.
- Shot 2 — **wide 84° low**: the ridge of riders runs the full frame width above a small figure.
- Shot 3 — **standard 47° medium**: grounded, physical, close enough that the shove and the glasses
  being pushed straight both read clearly.
- Shot 4 — **standard 47°, tightening on the rig** as the machine strains.
- Shot 5 — **short telephoto 29°, the film's only close-up**: background compresses to nothing, his
  face pops sharp, scarce on purpose. It is the only shot that holds his joy in close, and it lasts
  three seconds.

## Cast locks
- **RAJAN (`CHR_RAJAN`)** — in-story name **Rajan**. Late 30s, lean and slender, 5'10", medium-deep
  South Asian brown skin. Salt-and-pepper curls with height, swept back, grey at temples and
  hairline. Days-old grey-flecked stubble. **Rimless rectangular glasses, always on** — knocked
  askew by the push in shot 3 and pushed straight by his own hand, never lost; beaded with water in
  shots 5 and 6 but still on. Wardrobe: faded indigo canvas work-coat, knee-length, sand-scoured
  pale at the shoulders; dust-grey wrapped head-cloth loose at the neck; oatmeal linen shirt; heavy
  ochre canvas trousers strapped at the calf; scuffed brown leather palm-guards and boots.
  Brass-rimmed dust goggles pushed up on his forehead — **they sit above the glasses, never replace
  them**, and they are torn off by the water in shot 5. From 0:26 he is soaked through: coat gone
  dark with water, curls flattened and streaming, head-cloth heavy.
- **FLAG-RIDERS** — never individuated, never a face, never held on alone. Silhouetted figures in
  wind-cracked ochre and rust robes, mounted on long-legged sand-striders (four thin articulated
  legs, low slung body, a beast the height of two men — formidable in silhouette, warm and animal in
  shape, never monstrous). Each carries a banner pole in bleached crimson. **They appear in shots 2
  and 3 only, and are gone from the film after 0:18.**

## World rules
- The desert is fed by **sand-veins**: buried freshwater rivers running under the dunes, under
  pressure. Break the crust and water rises on its own — hard enough to throw a column of sand
  ahead of it. This is invented hydrology for an invented world; no real-world aquifer or drilling
  claim is asserted anywhere.
- **Nothing grows anywhere in this film.** No grass, reed, moss, algae, lichen, sapling, flower,
  crop, or greening of the sand — not on a bank, not in the distance, not in a single frame. The
  water is the only living thing on screen and it is unaccompanied. Abundance reads entirely as
  **volume, speed, height and noise of water**: the column's size, the river's width and pace, the
  weight of the rain coming down. The desert it crosses stays bare orange sand, start to finish.
- Each of the three waters is bigger than the last — a thread at 0:05, a channelled ribbon at 0:13,
  a full column and river at 0:27. That escalation is measured in water alone.
- Claim law is **flags**: a planted pole owns the water beneath it. That is the whole legal system,
  shown never explained.
- **Both takings happen fully on screen** — riders arrive, plant, and bury the water while Rajan
  watches, once at 0:06 and again at 0:14. The second is the harder of the two.
- **One physical contact exists in the entire film**: in shot 3 the lead rider sweeps his pole arm
  across Rajan's chest in passing and puts him flat in the sand, without stopping or looking back.
  No strike, no pile-on, no injury, no blood, no gloating, no second blow, nobody stands over him.
  The riders' register is indifference, not cruelty — they came for the ground, and he was in it.
  Nothing else in the film touches him. *(Supersedes the earlier "nobody ever touches him" rule, at
  Rajan's direction — the shove is story-critical and stays this contained.)*
- **The last ground is uncontested and stays uncontested.** After 0:18 nobody follows him, and the
  film ends with no rival, no claim, and no mark of one in frame. The final image is not a
  reconciliation and not a triumph over anyone — it is a man, water falling on him, and open land.
- **The black dune is unclaimed** because everyone who tried it failed. The ring of snapped,
  half-buried poles at its foot is the proof, and it is visible in shots 3 and 4 **only** — it is
  outside the frame in shots 5 and 6, and must not appear on any horizon there.
- Rajan's rig is one machine, hauled all film: canvas-and-brass bore-rig on a sledge, A-frame,
  hand-cranked flywheel, steel auger. Its shaft snaps at 0:23 and the broken shaft is what he
  drives down by hand in the climax. It is never repaired.
- No text, signage, glyphs, numerals or legible markings appear on any banner, machine, or surface
  anywhere in the film.
