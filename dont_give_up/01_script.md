FADE IN:


                                 ACT ONE


1.                                             0:00-0:06

EXT. THE SUNKEN FLATS - MIDDAY

Straight down onto an ocean of orange dunes. Dead centre,
a black bore-hole the size of a drum.

The ground DRUMS. Sand jumps clear of the crust in rings.

The hole ERUPTS. A column of dry sand fires straight up
at the lens -- ten storeys of it, boiling as it climbs,
mushrooming out flat at the top. Grit sheets across the
lens. A shockwave ring skates outward over four dune
crests at once.

The column hangs. Tips. Comes down as a slow brown rain
across the whole width of the desert.

Out of the falling dust: RAJAN, beside the hole, no
bigger than a thumbnail. Indigo work-coat, dust goggles
shoved up over rimless glasses, one hand still on the
crank of a canvas-and-brass bore-rig.

Under his boots the sand darkens. A wet ring spreads out
from the hole, and a thread of green water climbs into
the light.

He takes his hand off the crank.


                                 ACT TWO


2.                                             0:06-0:11

EXT. THE SUNKEN FLATS - CONTINUOUS

Low and wide. Rajan small at frame left with his green
pool. The dune ridge runs the full width behind him.

FLAG-RIDERS crest it. Silhouettes on long-legged
sand-striders, banner poles slung across their backs.
Ten. Then thirty. Then the whole ridge pours down the
face.

A pole CRACKS into the ground beside the hole. Another.
Then a row of them, snapping open in the wind.

Shovels. The green pool browns, shrinks, and is gone
under a rising heap of sand.

Rajan backs away, hauling the rig by its tow-rope. He
does not look at the riders. He looks at where the water
was.


3.                                             0:11-0:18

EXT. FAR DUNE FIELD - LATE AFTERNOON

New ground. Rajan walks the crank arm round with his
whole weight behind it. The rig SCREAMS. Sand kicks.

The bit drops. Water comes up fast and bright -- a ribbon
of green cutting its own channel downhill, widening as it
runs.

He is almost smiling.

THUNDER of legs. The striders come over the rise behind
him and down, spreading across the full width of the
frame.

The lead rider swings his pole low across Rajan's chest
in passing and puts him flat in the sand. The rider does
not look back. He drives the pole in beside the new
water.

From the ground, Rajan watches shovel after shovel go in.
The green ribbon thins. Browns. Stops.

He pushes his glasses straight. He gets up.

He looks back the way he came: a chain of flags stitched
across every dune to the horizon. One for every hole he
has ever opened.

He looks the other way.

Far out and alone stands a dune of black sand, and around
its foot a ring of snapped poles, half-buried, that
nobody came back for.

He picks up the tow-rope and walks toward it.


4.                                             0:18-0:24

EXT. THE BLACK DUNE - NIGHT

Moonlight with no colour in it. The rig sits lashed down
at the summit. Rajan hangs off the crank arm and walks it
round.

The auger bites. Black grit climbs the flights and pours
away down the slope.

Deeper. The A-frame shudders. The ropes hum.

Wind rises. Sand streams off the crest in long white
banners of its own.

He walks the crank faster. The rig SHRIEKS --

-- and the auger shaft SNAPS. The flywheel spins free.
Dry black dust pours out of the hole and keeps pouring.

Rajan goes down on his knees in it.


                                ACT THREE


5.                                             0:24-0:27

EXT. THE BLACK DUNE - FIRST LIGHT

Close on Rajan. Dust across both lenses. His breath
shaking.

He sets his shoulder against the broken shaft and drives
it down. Once. Again.

The ground BREATHES -- a deep note under him, felt more
than heard. The crust splits. Green-gold light climbs up
from below the frame line and crosses his face.

The water BREAKS. It comes up past him white and roaring
and tears the goggles off his head. He lets them go.

His face comes up into it. Open, streaming, lifted.


6.                                             0:27-0:30

EXT. THE BLACK DUNE - CONTINUOUS

Straight down onto black sand. Dead centre, the hole.

It ERUPTS. A column of water fires straight up at the
lens -- higher than the sand went, sunlit through, throwing
rainbows off its own spray.

It opens out, hangs, and comes down across the whole
width of the desert as shining rain. Below it the river
tears away over the orange dunes and runs for the
horizon. Open land, edge to edge, and nothing on it but
water.

The rain falls on Rajan. Soaked through, head back, arms
wide, turning slowly under it -- a small figure at the
foot of something far bigger than he is, laughing.

The column keeps climbing.

                                                FADE OUT.
