# craft-visual

Read by: cinematographer, storyboard-artist. Read `CRAFT.md` first.

## Founding principles

### The colour script comes before any shot

Before describing a single frame, decide the film's colour journey across the six nodes — as a
sentence per node. Cold and desaturated through node 4, warmth entering at the turn in node 5,
full colour by node 6, is a cliché precisely because it works; use it knowingly or break it
knowingly. Write the colour script into the script header so every downstream stage inherits it.

### Lighting carries theology in this workshop

Light is the workshop's most-used visual language, and it is worth using with precision rather
than reflex. Some distinctions worth holding:

- **Source matters more than intensity.** Where the light comes *from* is the storytelling.
  Light arriving from off-screen, from below the frame line, from behind a closed door, reads as
  something entering the scene. Light from an on-screen practical the character controls reads
  as something they made.
- **A single source beats a bright scene.** One motivated key with deep falloff will always look
  more filmic — and more like a picture book plate — than an evenly lit one.
- **Warmth is the arrival of grace; coldness is not evil, it is absence.** Avoid coding
  characters as villainous with light. The charter's world has no villains rendered with dread.

### Camera movement must be motivated

Every move answers "why now?" A push-in at a realisation. A slow rise as scale is revealed. A
handheld drift when the character's ground is unstable. Movement without reason reads as
restlessness and eats seconds a three-minute film cannot spare.

Seedance renders slow, single-intent moves far more reliably than compound ones. Prefer *one*
move per shot: push in, OR crane up, OR track left. Not a push-in that becomes a crane.

### Lens language

- Wide (14–24mm) — scale, isolation, the world dwarfing the person. Distorts faces; avoid close.
- Normal (35–50mm) — the honest eye. Default for anything emotional and mid-distance.
- Long (85–135mm) — compression, intimacy, the world falling away behind a face. The turn.

Naming a focal length in a prompt meaningfully changes what these models render. Always name it.

### Shot size rhythm

A three-minute film should not sit in one shot size. The reliable rhythm: establish wide, live in
mediums, spend the emotional turn in close, and return to wide for the closing image. When a
close-up is scarce, it is enormous.

### The picture-book quality

The charter asks for the taste and warmth of a children's picture book. Concretely, that means:
generous negative space, readable silhouettes, a limited palette per scene, texture over grime,
and light that flatters. It does not mean flat, childish, or low-detail — it is a *taste*, not a
budget. Epic scale and picture-book warmth coexist perfectly well.

### Storyboard panels are not asset renders

Asset prompts are deliberately neutral (see `craft-assets.md`). Storyboard panels are the
opposite: full mood, full colour grade, full lighting design. They are the key frames Seedance
anchors to. Every panel should look like a frame someone would pause the film on.

### Continuity is a visual discipline

Across six separately generated nodes, drift is the enemy. Lock and repeat verbatim: palette,
lighting quality and direction, film stock or render character, aspect ratio, and each
character's silhouette and wardrobe. Ambiguity in these is where the six nodes stop looking like
one film.
