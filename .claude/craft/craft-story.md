## Founding principles

These are the starting craft of the workshop. Retrospective lessons accumulate below them and
take precedence where they conflict.

### Shape of a three-minute film

180 seconds is not a feature film in miniature. It is one turn. One character, one want, one
obstacle, one change. Subplots kill it. A cast above three speaking parts kills it.

The six nodes map naturally onto a shape:

| Node | Seconds | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0–30 | Opening image + the world + who this person is when nothing is wrong |
| 2 | 30–60 | The disruption. Something is asked of them, or taken |
| 3 | 60–90 | The attempt. It goes wrong, or costs more than expected |
| 4 | 90–120 | The low point. The thing they were relying on fails |
| 5 | 120–150 | The turn. Grace arrives, or is finally accepted |
| 6 | 150–180 | The new state. The closing image rhymes with the opening one |

Deviate when the story demands it — but deviate deliberately, and say why in the beat sheet.

### The opening image and the closing image must rhyme

The single strongest structural move available in a short. Same location, same object, same
framing — changed meaning. The audience feels the whole arc in one cut without being told.

### Weave, never preach

The charter says it: *inspired by* the Word, truth woven in rather than preached. In practice:

- A character who states the theme aloud has stolen it from the audience. Let the image, the
  action, or the cost carry it.
- The most reliable Biblical structure in a short is not a quoted verse — it is a **pattern**:
  undeserved rescue, a debt paid by someone else, light that persists where it should not,
  a name spoken to someone who thought they were forgotten, the last made first.
- Scripture quoted on screen works only when a character would genuinely speak it in that
  moment, and even then, once per film at most.
- The scripture anchor belongs in the script header, so everyone downstream knows what the
  film is *about* underneath. It does not have to appear in the film.

### Emotional truth over incident

Three minutes cannot hold much plot, but it can hold one true feeling completely. When choosing
between "more happens" and "what happens lands harder," always choose the second.

### Dialogue

Sparse. A short film is a visual form and Seedance renders performance better than it renders
conversation. Aim for under 12 lines in the whole film; several strong shorts have two. Every
line must do work no image could do. No line explains what we just watched.

### Family audience is a craft constraint, not a limitation

Menace without dread: threat is legible through stakes and the character's face, not through
gore or startle. A creature can be enormous, strange, and unsettling in silhouette and still be
rendered with the warmth of a picture book. Fear resolves — within the film, and quickly.

### What the reviewers are for

The head writer is not outvoted by the room. Reviewers surface problems and propose fixes; the
head writer integrates. But `scripture-guardian` holds a veto on blocking theological findings,
and `fact-checker` holds a veto on real-world falsehood presented as truth. Those two are not
negotiable — everything else is craft judgement.

### Grounded in truth while wholly imaginative

The charter permits any world. It does not permit a false claim wearing the costume of a fact.
Invented physics in an invented world is fine. A real mechanism, a real place, a real historical
event, a real scriptural citation — stated as real — must be correct. When a story needs a real
fact bent, either invent the thing outright or let a character be wrong on purpose.
