# VISION.md

## The mission

Every film made here carries a truth from the Bible into a story that stands on its own —
grand, imaginative, and open to anyone, whatever they believe. The model is C.S. Lewis and
Narnia: Aslan is a lion before he is anything else, and the story he lives is a wonderful
adventure long before it is recognized, if it ever is, as the story of Christ. Nobody has
to know the second thing to enjoy the first.

This is not a debate the film wins or loses. Nobody is being persuaded. The film is a gift
— a genuinely good story that happens to be built, load-bearing beam by load-bearing beam,
out of what the Bible teaches. If the intended viewer never notices the beams, the film
still worked. If they do notice, it rewards a second and third watch.

## Where the stories come from

A Bible passage, a pattern across several, or something Rajan takes from a sermon or his
own reading — any of these can be the seed. What they all become, before a script gets
written, is a *pattern*: a shape of events and relationships, not a specific set of names.
"An undeserved rescue." "A father who runs to meet the one who left." "A small, unlikely
thing that turns out to hold everything." The pattern is what survives translation into
any world, genre, or scale.

## How the symbol works

Symbols carry weight without saying the word. A lion, a father who never stops watching
the road, a light that outlasts the dark, a meal that costs the host everything, a return
from somewhere no one returns from — these are tools of the craft here, used the way Lewis
used them: specific, felt, physical, never labeled. Name the animal, the person, the
object. Never name the doctrine.

## The line that still holds

The story never turns into a lecture, a chapter-and-verse citation, or a church service.
It doesn't announce itself as religious, and it doesn't need the literal words *God*,
*Jesus*, *the Bible*, or *Christian* to do its work — a story that needs to say the word
hasn't found its symbol yet. Real-world religious institutions, denominational branding,
and literal places of worship stay out for the same reason a fantasy world doesn't run on
real-world brand names: this is an invented world carrying a true pattern, not a
recreation of a real one.

## Who it's for

Anyone. A viewer of any faith or none should be able to watch and simply enjoy a good
story. Someone who knows the Bible well should be able to watch the same film and feel
something click. Both experiences are the film succeeding — neither is required for the
other.
