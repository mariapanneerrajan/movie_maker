## BEAT SHEET

**LOGLINE**
When a garbage giant rises from the Cooum and the city's armoured response force empties every
weapon into it without effect, a slender conservancy worker refuses the exo-suit they offer him,
wades into the black water for five sorted things, and aims for the one seam that armour was never
covering.

**SCRIPTURE ANCHOR (header only — never spoken, never quoted on screen)**
1 Samuel 17:38–40 — David refuses Saul's armour and chooses five smooth stones from the brook; read
with 17:45, *not with sword and spear*. Two supporting patterns, also unspoken: **1 Samuel
17:34–37** — he has already done this small, with the lion and the bear, which is why he can do it
large; and **Psalm 118:22 / Matthew 21:42** — the stone the builders rejected. The fifth thing is
the one piece of refuse that fits none of his five bins.

**THEME (for the room, not for the film)**
Faith is not the absence of fear — it is the small, clean, careful act done while afraid, by the
person nobody sent for. And the giant is made of what the city threw away, so it can only be unmade
the way it was made: one small thing at a time, by everyone.

**INTENDED FEELING AS THE CREDITS LAND**
Awe at the scale, relief at the fall, and a lift — then the quiet, pleasant sting of being
implicated. That the wrapper in your hand tomorrow morning is not nothing.

**THE INVENTED PHYSICS, STATED ONCE SO IT IS NEVER ARBITRARY**
Mixed waste, wet-packed and fermenting under compaction, welds itself into one mass — rot is the
glue. The giants are single unsorted knots. *Force only feeds them:* every round fired becomes more
mass, and they wear the spent shells as new plating. But a thing that has been **cleaned, dried and
sorted** carries no rot and cannot be bonded to. Introduce one such thing at the breathing seam —
the intake the giant must open to feed, the one place plating can never cover — and the mass has
nowhere to bind except like to like. It separates. It sorts itself. And a sorted mass cannot stand.

**STRUCTURAL NOTE — one deliberate deviation.** craft-story's node 3 is "the attempt, by the
protagonist." Here node 3 is **the city's attempt**, and the protagonist's job in it is to be waved
aside. That is not a dodge; it is the story. David's exclusion is the engine of David's story, and
if Rajan gets a swing at the giant in minute one we lose both the David shape and the "unranked,
dismissed" rendering the seed's "little boy" was reconciled into. His attempt begins at 2:00 and is
the only attempt he gets.

**THE FIFTH STONE**
*First draft — head writer*

| **SETTING** | Chennai, India. Near future — perhaps fifteen years on. Solar-film awnings, drone streetlamps, magnet-sled sorting yards. The city is still entirely itself. |
| **THEME** | Faith overcomes fear; the giant is made of what we threw away |

**ARUL** — late 30s, lean and slender, salt-and-pepper curls, thin rimless rectangular glasses.

```
[NODE 1 · 0:00–0:30]

EXT. MARINA BEACH - PRE-DAWN

0:00-0:06 — Grey-blue light over the Bay of Bengal. The tide line
runs with litter: bottle caps, torn netting, foam cups. The
lighthouse stands off to the south. Somewhere behind the city, a
drone lamp is still burning.

-- "THE FIRST WRAPPER" --

A boy's hand opens. A mango-yellow foil wrapper drops out of it and
takes the wind. KAVI, 9, is already running on. He does not look
back. He does not think about it at all. The wrapper skitters and
cartwheels down the wet sand and stops.

0:06-0:12 —

-- "THE PICKER" --

A long-handled picker comes into the frame and takes the wrapper,
neatly, the way a heron takes a fish. ARUL straightens up. Corporation
greens, sleeves rolled, glasses fogging at the edges from the sea air.
Behind him a small five-bin handcart: green, blue, grey, white, amber,
each lid hand-lettered. A canvas pouch hangs at his hip, lettered with
a single question mark.

He turns the wrapper over. Foil one side, plastic the other, a thing
that belongs to none of the five. He tucks it into the pouch and moves
on. Two joggers pass through him like he is weather.

0:12-0:22 —

-- "THE LITTLE KNOT" --

Under the cart, something moves. A clot of wet litter the size of a
football, welded together out of rot and rag and plastic film, hunching
itself along the sand, dragging a straw and a lid into its own body as
it goes. Arul does not flinch. He has seen a thousand of these.

He takes a clear plastic bottle from the blue bin, holds it under the
little wash-jet on the cart, wipes it dry on his sleeve, and drops it
into the knot.

The knot shudders. And comes apart. Not violently — it RESOLVES, the
way a knot resolves when you find the right thread, unspooling into
five neat little rills across the sand. Plastic. Metal. Glass. Paper.
Wet. Five small tidy heaps.

Arul sweeps them into the five bins with a hand brush. He is thinking
about breakfast.

0:22-0:26 —

-- "THE POUCH" --

He pats the pouch at his hip flat. Whatever is in there, there is no
bin for it, and he carries it anyway.

0:26-0:30 —

-- "THE FIRST TREMOR" --

Far out past the surf, the water shivers in a wide ring. Every gull on
the beach goes up at once. Arul is bent over another bottle cap and
does not turn around.
```

```
[NODE 2 · 0:30–1:00]

EXT. THE COOUM RIVER, AT NAPIER BRIDGE - DAWN

0:30-0:40 — Black water, flat as tar, under a lightening sky. The
clock tower of Chennai Central stands beyond it. Traffic crawls over
the bridge.

-- "THE COOUM STANDS UP" --

The river bulges. A shoulder breaks the surface — corrugated sheet
metal lapped like scales, bound with fishing net and cable. Then
another. Then a head: a slab of pressed refuse with two lorry
headlamps set in it for eyes and a bus grille for a jaw. It rises and
rises, black water pouring off it in sheets, sixty metres of the city's
own leavings standing up out of its own river. Torn hoardings hang off
its arms like banners. Tyres for knuckles.

It is enormous and strange and, somehow, sad. It is not roaring. It is
BREATHING — and where it breathes, at the base of its throat, the
plating parts into a long horizontal seam that opens and closes with
each breath.

0:40-0:47 —

-- "WHAT IT SWALLOWS" --

It walks up the bank. The seam draws in. A tea cart goes up off the
pavement and in. A hoarding peels off a wall and in. An empty MTC bus
lifts by its roof, wheels turning slowly in the air, and in. A
yellow-and-black auto-rickshaw goes up sideways with its indicator
still blinking, patient, orange, on and off, all the way in.

People run. Nobody is caught by it. The street is emptying like a
bathtub.

0:47-0:52 —

-- "THE BOY IN THE NETTING" --

A sheet of loose netting sweeps the pavement rail and lifts. KAVI is
in it, tangled to the waist, going up fast, hands scrabbling at the
mesh. He is not hurt. He is very, very frightened. The netting settles
against the giant's shoulder and holds him there, sixty metres up,
like a caught kite.

0:52-0:57 —

-- "THE FLYOVER STRIDE" --

The giant steps over Anna Salai and puts one foot down on the Anna
flyover. The deck bows. Cars have been abandoned mid-lane with their
doors open. It strides along the flyover as though it were a kerb,
hoardings shedding off its back and drifting down over the traffic
like enormous leaves.

0:57-1:00 —

-- "THE SECOND HORIZON" --

Fifteen kilometres north, on the kuppa medu skyline, one of the garbage
hills stands up too.

On Marina Beach, Arul has finally turned around. His picker is still
in his hand.
```

```
[NODE 3 · 1:00–1:30]

EXT. RANGANATHAN STREET, T. NAGAR - MORNING

1:00-1:08 — The narrowest, busiest street in the city, and it is empty.
Silk-shop hoardings four storeys tall. Hanging banners. Shutters half
down over abandoned displays.

-- "THE ARMOURED LINE" --

Twenty exo-frames fill the street mouth, shoulder to shoulder — three
metres each, rail-cannon slung, hydraulics venting. Drones stack up
above them. CAPT. MEERA stands out in front, helmet off, watching the
top of the street where the giant's shadow is coming.

1:08-1:14 —

-- "BEHIND THE LINE" --

Arul arrives at a jog, pushing the handcart, greens soaked to the
thigh. He is out of breath and completely certain, and the combination
makes him hard to listen to. He gets in front of Meera.

                    ARUL
     Every round you fire -- it keeps.

Meera does not look at him. A crewman's arm comes across Arul's chest
and walks him backwards.

                    MEERA
     Behind the line.

Arul is put behind the line. He stands there holding a picker among
twenty rail-cannon.

1:14-1:22 —

-- "EVERYTHING AT ONCE" --

The giant turns into the top of Ranganathan Street, and it barely
fits. Meera drops her hand.

Everything fires. The street goes white. Rail slugs, drone fire, foam
charges — a wall of it, dead centre, hammering into the plating.
Hoardings shred off the giant's chest and fall burning-slow, like
paper. It is the most impressive thing anyone in this city has ever
seen.

1:22-1:30 —

-- "IT KEEPS" --

The smoke clears.

The giant is BIGGER. Every spent slug has gone into it and stayed —
they lie across its chest in a bright new layer of plating, seam upon
seam, welded on by the impact that was meant to kill it. It is
armoured now in exactly what they shot at it.

Not one round has touched the breathing seam at the base of its
throat. Nothing ever will. It is the one place plating cannot cover,
and the plating is now four inches thicker everywhere else.

It leans down and takes the silk-shop awnings off the street, one long
inhale, and moves on toward the river.

Arul, behind the line, watching. He has understood something and there
is nobody left who will hold still for it.
```

```
EXT. COOUM RIVERBANK, BELOW NAPIER BRIDGE - MORNING

1:30-1:37 —

-- "THE LINE BREAKS" --

Exo-frames coming back fast along the bank, out of ammunition, venting

1:37-1:45 —

Meera turns and finds Arul standing there. Still holding the picker.

end of options.

She swings the hatch of the last spare frame open with her boot.

                    MEERA
     Then you wear it.

1:45-1:52 —

-- "I CAN'T WORK IN THIS" --

Arul climbs in. The frame closes around him. It is beautiful — plated,

He tries to close the gauntlet on something small. The fingers are

He works the hatch and steps out of it, back into the mud in his wet

                    ARUL

     Work.

Arul sits down on the kerb because his legs have decided it. He takes
```

```
[NODE 5 · 2:00–2:30]

EXT. THE COOUM - CONTINUOUS

2:00-2:06 —

-- "PAST THE SUITS" --

Arul walks the row of kneeling exo-frames. Twenty open hatches, twenty
cannon, sixty million rupees of armour lying in the mud on either side

He takes none of it. He picks his picker out of the gutter, checks

2:06-2:12 —

-- "INTO THE COOUM" --

At the water's edge he stops. The Cooum is black and still and it
smells like every bad decision this city ever made.

He is afraid. It is all over him — the breathing, the jaw, the hands.
He does not pretend otherwise. He looks up once, briefly, at nothing
anyone else can see.

                    ARUL
          (barely audible)
     Help me find them.

He goes in. Knee. Waist. Chest.

2:12-2:20 —

-- "FIVE THINGS" --

He works by touch, both arms under the surface, face turned up and
away, the way he has stood at a sorting line for twenty years. His
hand closes on something and he brings it up.

ONE — a clear plastic bottle.
TWO — a flattened steel tumbler.
THREE — a glass jar, unbroken.
FOUR — a block of newspaper, sodden, that he squeezes out in his fist.

He wades to the bank and washes each one under the jet until the water
running off it is clean, and dries each one on his sleeve, and sets
them in a row on the mud. It takes him four seconds and it is the most
careful thing in the film.

FIVE — floating past his chest, turning slowly: a mango-yellow foil
wrapper.

He takes it. He washes it. He dries it. He folds it into a small tight
square between his fingers and holds it in his fist.

2:20-2:27 —

-- "FOUR THROWS" --

He walks straight out under the giant. It is a man the size of a
thumbnail standing under a weather system.

He throws the bottle. It strikes the new plating and spins away.
He throws the tumbler. Deflected off a tyre knuckle.
He throws the jar. Gone into the netting.
He throws the newspaper block. It falls short and lands in the water.

The giant looks down. Two lorry headlamps swing and settle on him.
Arul does not run. He has one thing left in his hand and it is a
sweet wrapper.

2:27-2:30 —

-- "THE OPEN HAND" --

The giant leans down over him. At the base of its throat the plating
parts — the seam, opening wide, drawing in air and dust and the whole
morning.

Arul does not throw.

He holds his fist up over his head. And opens it.

The small folded square lifts off his palm, turns once, and is
inhaled.

The giant stops.
```

```
[NODE 6 · 2:30–3:00]

EXT. THE COOUM - CONTINUOUS

2:30-2:38 —

-- "THE SORTING" --

Absolute silence. Sixty metres of standing refuse holds still with one
foot off the ground.

Then, from the seam, a line of clean light runs up through it and out

Metal follows in a bright ribbon, the spent slugs going with it.
Glass comes off in a slow glittering veil that catches the sun.
Paper leaves it in a snowfall of a hundred thousand sheets, drifting

Five streams. Five hills. Nothing standing.

It falls without a crash. It sits down.

2:38-2:42 —

-- "COUGHED LOOSE" --

The netting lowers with the last of it and lays KAVI down on the mud
on his feet, dirty, blinking, entirely unhurt. Beside him the
auto-rickshaw settles upright, indicator still blinking, on, off, on.

Arul, ten metres away, sits down in the shallows. He is laughing and
he cannot stop and he is also crying a little, and he is filthy.

2:42-2:47 —

-- "BAGS" --

Meera, on the bank, looking at five clean hills where a giant was.

She pulls off one gauntlet. Then the other. She walks down to the

and turns it over, and looks at it, and puts it in a sack.

                    MEERA
     Bags.

Twenty pilots climb out of twenty exo-frames.

2:47-2:51 —

-- "THE CITY BENDS DOWN" --

RANGANATHAN STREET: shutters going up. A shopkeeper drags a crate of
sorted glass out into the road and starts filling it. A boy sweeps
foil into a sack.

THE ANNA FLYOVER: people out of their cars, walking the lanes, picking

MARINA BEACH: a line of people the whole width of the sand, moving

Five colours of sack, everywhere, all at once.

2:51-2:55 —

-- "THE RIVER CLEARS" --

The Cooum, from the same spot at Napier Bridge, later — weeks, months.
Green water. Green banks. Two herons standing in it. Children on the
bridge above, leaning over the rail to look down at a river that is
worth looking at.

On the north horizon, the kuppa medu hills are being taken down and
rebuilt as five neat coloured mountains, and the whole skyline is
covered in people.

2:55-3:00 —

EXT. MARINA BEACH - DAWN

-- "THE LAST WRAPPER" --

The same grey-blue light. The same wet sand, the same lighthouse, the
same frame as the first shot of the film. The sand is clean.

A mango-yellow foil wrapper takes the wind and skitters down toward
the tide line.

KAVI runs it down. Catches it. Turns it over in his fingers — foil one
side, plastic the other, a thing that belongs to none of the five —
and drops it into the canvas pouch on the hip of the man standing
beside him. Arul's hand comes down on the boy's head.

Behind them the beach fills. Another wrapper lifts off the sand and
goes.

Three different hands reach for it.
```

## HEAD WRITER'S NOTES ON THE SEED

1. **"Destroying different buildings and bridges."** The giant *steps over*, *bows*, *sheds
   hoardings across* and *swallows the streetscape* — but no real, named Chennai landmark is
   demolished. Charter reason: collapsing Chennai Central or the Anna flyover with an empty city
   underneath invites the audience to do the arithmetic on casualties, and the moment they do, the
   film has dread in it. Craft reason: an emptied street with the doors of abandoned cars hanging
   open is more frightening and far warmer than rubble, and it keeps the giant *sad* rather than
   *evil* — which the ending needs, because the city has to recognise the giant as its own doing
   and not as an enemy it defeated. If Rajan wants visible structural damage, the safest place is
   the Anna flyover deck bowing under the foot, already written, and it can be pushed further
   without changing anything else.

2. **The second giant never falls on screen.** Established at 0:57, being *unbuilt by the whole
   city* at 2:51. Deliberate: Arul brings down one giant, the city brings down the other, and the
   second one is the whole point of the ending. It also protects node 6 from becoming an action
   beat when it needs to be a turning of the heart.

3. **A flag for the room, not a problem.** The mechanism means Arul cannot know it will work at
   sixty metres — he has only ever done it to a football-sized knot. That gap *is* the faith, and
   it is deliberately not closed with a line of dialogue. If a reviewer asks for a line where he
   explains why it will work, the head writer declines: the second he is certain, he is a
   technician and not a man with faith, and the film loses its spine.

4. **Kavi's containment.** He is a stake and a mirror, not an agent. He never solves anything. He
   gets the film's opening careless gesture and its closing careful one — the entire arc of the
   city compressed into one nine-year-old — and he speaks one word.

5. **Watch item for `fact-checker`.** Geography is real and load-bearing: the Cooum empties into
   the Bay of Bengal beside Napier Bridge at the north end of Marina; Chennai Central's clock tower
   stands near the Cooum; Anna Salai carries the Anna flyover; Ranganathan Street is in T. Nagar;
   the kuppa medu hills are north of the city. Marina faces east, so all dawn light comes off the
   sea — the opening and closing images depend on that being right. No brand names appear anywhere;
   the wrapper is described by colour only.
