> **GATE 5 — HOW THE FOUR-FRAME INSERT IS EXECUTED. Read this; it is the answer to the question that
> was asked.**
>
> **It cannot be done in a Seedance prompt and must not be attempted there.** A four-frame cut is
> 0.16 seconds. No video model will place a 0.16s cutaway on command, and every attempt will produce
> either a morph, a glitch, or a full second of the wrong image.
>
> **Do not put the insert in the node-5 prompt at all.** Node 5 renders 2:16.2–2:16.9 as one
> continuous shot of Rajan's hand closing.

> (0:03.3–0:04.0) is Kavi's hand opening on wet sand at 85mm, camera 1.1 m, framed to this exact spec
> for this exact purpose. Take four consecutive frames from the moment his fingers finish relaxing

> **HOW THIS READS AS A DECISION AND NOT AS A MECHANISM JAMMING — four instructions, all binding:**
>
> **(1) THE PLATES CLOSE EARLY.** They come together BEFORE the block reaches them, not on contact. A
> jam happens at the moment of contact. A decision happens before it. This is the whole difference
> and it is entirely a matter of timing.
> **(2) ONE SMOOTH MOVEMENT, THEN ABSOLUTE STILLNESS.** No shudder, no vibration, no grinding, no
> stutter, no judder, no sparks, no strain, no recoil. The seam closes the way a hand closes, and
> then nothing in the frame moves at all.
> **(3) THE SEAM IS WIDE OPEN AND STILL BEFORE THE THROW.** It stops its breathing cycle and holds
> open — attention — and then it closes. Attention, then act, is the grammar of a decision.
> **(4) THE BLOCK DOES NOT BOUNCE. IT DROPS.** It stops dead against flat sheet metal and falls
> straight down, vertically, out of frame bottom. A bounce is mechanics. A vertical drop is refusal.
>
> The frame holds the seam and the sky above it, with the block arriving small and pale from
> below-left. The giant's head is NOT in this frame. The jaw is not in this frame. Nothing about this
> shot is a face.

> **SEVEN SECONDS, FIVE SHOTS. This is the beat that will not generate as one continuous flow and
> must not be carried to gate 5 in that form.** Five simultaneous materially-distinct streams
> separating out of a collapsing sixty-metre mass in one shot is a compound instruction; Seedance
> will average it into an undifferentiated debris cloud — which is the exact read (destruction) the
> shot exists to avoid.
>
> **THE FIVE STREAMS ARE SEPARATED IN SPACE, NOT IN TIME.** Plastic and metal live inside shot C, as
> the outward peel. Paper gets its own shot, D, because it is the picture-book image the whole film
> has been walking toward and it is the most generable element in the sequence. Glass and wet waste
> are arrivals inside the pan, E. Nothing in the writer's prose is lost; every stream is assigned to
> a frame.
>
> **TWO RULES GOVERN ALL FIVE SHOTS:**
> **EVERYTHING RISES BEFORE IT FALLS.** Rising-then-settling is the grammar of release; collapsing is
> the grammar of destruction.
> **NOTHING LANDS ON ANYONE.** People stand untouched inside the falling paper, visibly.
> No fire, no explosion, no smoke, no shockwave, nothing thrown toward camera, no dust cloud.

**1. The four-frame flash-cut at 2:16.** Cannot be prompted. **Solved by editorial harvest** from shot

not let gate 4 re-frame it.

**2. SEGREGATE AT SOURCE — my recommendation, as asked.** **Plate treatment, not generation.** Generate
the board *blank* in both instances (`"a blank sun-bleached pale Corporation-green painted metal
hoarding panel, no text, no lettering, no writing"`), composite the lettering as a flat tracked 2D
plate in post, in a real font, with matched bleaching and matched drift-shadow at the bottom. The words
never enter a prompt at any gate. This requires two things of me and I have supplied both: the board is
**planar to camera within 15°** and **the camera is locked** in both shots, so the track is trivial; and
the framing is **identical at 0:12.4 and 2:56.4**, so the same plate serves both with only a
cleanliness pass between them. This converts the film's one legible-text liability into the film's
third rhyme frame, at no cost.

**3. The question-mark glyph.** Borderline — a single simple glyph is worth attempting, and I still
recommend keeping it. **Prompt it as a shape, not a character**: `"a single hand-painted dark
curl-and-dot mark on faded canvas."` Have a composite plate ready as a backup, using the same tracked
treatment as item 2. It appears in three shots (0:10, 2:03, 2:57), all at the same framing, so one
plate covers all three if generation fails.

**4. The 2:54 dissolve.** Preferred solution editorial; fallback in-node. See the boxed note at 2:54.0.
Gate-5 decision.

**5. Node 1, 0:21 — the small resolve has the same failure mode as the big one, at 1/100 scale.** Five
simultaneous streams out of one mass in one shot, which is precisely the instruction that will average
into a debris cloud. **This is a genuine find and it was not in my round-1 review.** Solved at shot 1.12
by the same principle used at 2:31 — rise before separation, radial separation *in space*, five named
directions, named order. The two resolves must be storyboarded against each other as one event at two
scales; if node 1's fails, node 6's payoff has nothing to rhyme with.

**6. Prompt hygiene — the word "mouth" is in the script four times** and every instance sits near the
creature: "outfall mouth" (0:30), "the mouth of Ranganathan Street" (1:26), "the mouth of the Cooum"

**1. `terracotta brown` is gone and I do not miss it.** `dark loam brown` is the better string anyway —
"loam" and "potting soil" are anchors a model actually knows, where "terracotta" was a pigment name
doing double duty as a material name. The intent survives whole: the ugliest stream reads as soil, and
at 2:52 it is the one with green on it.

theirs. That is the film in one line, and it is statable in every node prompt.
