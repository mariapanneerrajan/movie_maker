# Round 1 — scripture-guardian (VETO HOLDER)

**Draft reviewed:** `_draft_v1.md`
**VERDICT: REVISE**

**SCRIPTURE ANCHOR AS WRITTEN:** A disregarded man refuses the armour of the powerful and brings
down what force only fed — not by throwing but by opening his hand — so that the city is rescued by
a gift it refused and did not earn; but the closing thirty seconds hand the finishing of that
rescue back to the crowd's own labour, and the head writer's notes say that second half is "the
whole point."

---

## FINDINGS

### 1. [BLOCKING] Node 6 (2:47–2:55), with HEAD WRITER'S NOTE 2 and the beat sheet's THEME line

**PROBLEM:** The film establishes the giant as the embodied consequence of the city's own moral
carelessness ("a thousand small carelessnesses," "it smells like every bad decision this city ever
made," the audience "implicated"), and then resolves that consequence by the city's own corporate
effort — "it can only be unmade the way it was made: one small thing at a time, by everyone," with
the second giant explicitly "brought down by the city" and the head writer naming that as the point
of the ending. Once the refuse is freighted as sin, the sack becomes a sacrament and the film's
final assertion is that a people cancels its own guilt by its own good behaviour. That is
works-righteousness in its plainest available form, and it is the last thing the audience is told.

**SCRIPTURE:** Jeremiah 2:22 — "For though thou wash thee with nitre, and take thee much soap, yet
thine iniquity is marked before me, saith the Lord GOD." And Ephesians 2:8–9 — "For by grace are ye
saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: Not of works, lest any man
should boast."

**FIX:** Do not cut the cleanup — the seed requires it and stewardship is a true and commanded thing
(Genesis 2:15). Re-order its *meaning* so it reads as response, not payment. Three concrete moves,
**any two of which clear this**:

- **(a)** Strike the beat sheet's "it can only be unmade the way it was made" and replace with "it
  was unmade by one man's open hand; the city's work is what gratitude looks like." The
  screenplay's own chronology is already correct — the giant is down at 2:38, "Bags" is at 2:47 —
  so the fix is to stop the notes contradicting the film.
- **(b)** The second giant must not fall to human labour. Let what Arul released keep spreading: at
  2:51 the northern giant comes apart on the horizon *before* the crowd reaches it, and the people
  on the skyline are gathering what has already been sorted, not sorting it. The whole city is then
  working *inside* a rescue it did not accomplish.
- **(c)** At 2:47, have Meera pick up the bottle **with the bare hand Arul showed her** — the
  gesture is imitation of a gift, not repayment of a debt. Keep the gauntlet coming off; it is the
  best image in the beat.

### 2. [STRONG] Node 5, 2:06–2:20 — "Help me find them," then he finds them himself

**PROBLEM:** The only God-ward moment in the film is answered by Arul's own twenty years of skill:
the script says he works "the way he has stood at a sorting line for twenty years," and brings up
four things by touch. Nothing arrives that he did not fetch. A prayer that can be deleted without
changing a single frame teaches, by implication, that prayer changes nothing — and it leaves the
film's answer to "what saves this city" as *correct technique, finally applied by the expert nobody
listened to*. That is a fine secular story and it is not the David pattern, whose whole narrative
purpose is that the outcome is not the sling's.

**SCRIPTURE:** James 1:17 — "Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down
from the Father of lights." 1 Samuel 17:47 — "the LORD saveth not with sword and spear: for the
battle is the LORD's."

**FIX:** No dialogue added; one shot changed. He asks for five. He finds **four** — and the fourth
is the sodden newspaper, the one that falls short. Then he stops, out of things to reach for, and
the fifth **comes to him**: the wrapper turning slowly past his chest, unsought. The draft already
half-does this ("floating past his chest") — commit to it and let the camera register that he did
not go and get it. The one he did not earn is the one that works. That converts the film's answer
from competence to grace, perfects the Psalm 118:22 anchor (the rejected thing is *given*, not
sourced), and costs four seconds of staging.

### 3. [STRONG] Node 3, 1:08–1:14 — "out of breath and completely certain"

**PROBLEM:** The head writer's Note 3 argues, correctly, that Arul's inability to know the method
scales to sixty metres *is* the faith. But the draft undercuts that argument itself: node 1
demonstrates the mechanism working, and node 3 stages Arul as "completely certain." Certainty about
the diagnosis and certainty about the cure are different things, and the draft currently blurs them
— which makes him a vindicated technician rather than a frightened man acting past the end of his
knowledge.

**SCRIPTURE:** Hebrews 11:1 — "faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things
not seen." Hebrews 11:8 — Abraham "went out, not knowing whither he went."

**FIX:** Let him be certain of the diagnosis and unsure of the cure. "Every round you fire — it
keeps" stays, delivered with total conviction. But when Meera says "Then you wear it," his face
should not say *finally*; it should say he has just been handed the thing he was afraid someone
would ask him to do. Cut "completely certain" to "out of breath, and sure of only half of it."

### 4. [SUGGESTION] Node 3, 1:22–1:30 — "It is armoured now in exactly what they shot at it"

**PROBLEM:** Not a problem — an unclaimed asset. The strongest theological image in the draft is
that force applied to the consequence of sin *increases* it, and nobody in the beat sheet has
noticed. Left unnamed in the header, downstream stages may flatten it into a stock "weapons don't
work" beat.

**SCRIPTURE:** Romans 5:20 — "the law entered, that the offence might abound." Romans 7:8 — "sin,
taking occasion by the commandment."

**FIX:** Add to the SCRIPTURE ANCHOR header, unspoken like the rest: "Romans 5:20 — what is fired at
the mass becomes the mass." One line, and every downstream agent knows to protect the beat.

### 5. [SUGGESTION] Standing production note — the religious-imagery veto, downstream exposure

**PROBLEM:** The draft itself contains no breach. Every landmark named is secular and the upward
glance is correctly written as "at nothing anyone else can see." But two prompts implied by the
draft will very likely generate a breach at gates 3–4: (i) "Silk-shop hoardings four storeys tall"
and "hanging banners" on Ranganathan Street — real T. Nagar hoardings routinely carry deity and
festival imagery, and an image model asked for them will supply it; (ii) Marina Beach promenade,
which carries memorial statuary, and "statuary" is vetoed outright.

**FIX:** Extend the standing production note with two explicit exclusions: silk-shop and street
hoardings are typography, textile and pattern only, no figures; and Marina frames stay on sand, surf
and lighthouse, promenade statuary out of frame. Also worth recording for the head writer: the veto
covers buildings, shrines, icons, statues and festival imagery — it does **not** forbid light. A
shaft of light, a break in cloud, a source Arul looks toward is available and permitted, and would
strengthen finding 2 at no cost to the constraint.

---

## On the head writer's Note 3 — I back him, without reservation

His refusal to close the sixty-metre gap with a line of explanation is not a weakness the room
should talk him out of; it is the only reason this film has a subject. The moment Arul can account
for why it will work, faith becomes confidence and the story becomes an engineering anecdote.
Hebrews 11:8 is exactly this shape — "he went out, not knowing whither he went." Finding 3 above is
not an attack on that argument; it is the observation that the draft has not yet honoured its own
position, because "completely certain" and a fully demonstrated mechanism at node 1 have already
partly closed the gap the head writer says he is protecting.

## What is genuinely right here, so it survives revision

The dialogue is exemplary. Eight lines, and not one states the theme — "I can't work in this"
carries 1 Samuel 17:39 as pure practical complaint and never once reaches for meaning. There is no
sermon in this draft in either act, and craft-story's warning about the theme-stating line does not
apply anywhere in it. **The failure mode here is under-weaving, not preaching, and the head writer
should not be pushed one inch toward the overt to fix it.**

Three things I would defend against any other reviewer: the giant rendered *sad* rather than evil;
the open hand replacing the throw at 2:27 (a rescue *given*, not won — Romans 5:8, "while we were
yet sinners"); and the pouch with the question mark, carried all film, holding the thing that fits
none of the five bins. That last one is the best theological instrument in the script — Psalm
118:22, "the stone which the builders refused is become the head stone of the corner" — and finding
2's fix exists mainly to let it land at full strength.

## What clears the veto

**Finding 1 only.** Any two of its three sub-moves, or an equivalent of the head writer's own
devising, provided the film's final thirty seconds no longer assert that the city finishes its own
rescue. Findings 2 and 3 are coupled to it: if 1 is cleared and 2 is not, the film's answer to "what
saves this city" is still competence, and I will raise it again at round 2.
