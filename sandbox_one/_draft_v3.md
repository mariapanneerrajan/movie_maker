## BEAT SHEET

**LOGLINE** *(approved at gate 1, verbatim)*
When a garbage giant rises from the Cooum and the city's armoured response force empties every
weapon into it without effect, a slender conservancy worker refuses the exo-suit they offer him,
wades into the black water for five sorted things, and aims for the one seam that armour was never
covering.

**SCRIPTURE ANCHOR — header only. Not quoted, not paraphrased, not spoken.**
- **1 Samuel 17:38–40**, with **17:34–37** (the lion and the bear — node 1's knot) and **17:45**.
- **Romans 5:20** — *where sin abounded.* Force applied to the consequence of sin **increases it.**
  Node 3 is not "weapons don't work." It is **they feed it.** Do not flatten.
- **Psalm 118:22 / Matthew 21:42** — the fifth thing fits none of the five bins. **Theme only. Never
  the mechanism.**
- **ORDER OF OPERATIONS, binding: rescue first, labour second.** Nobody in this film brings a giant
  down — not the response force, not the crowd, not Rajan. Rajan opens his hand and the giant takes the
  thing itself. The city's labour walks alongside the rescue and never ahead of it.

**THEME (for the room, not the film)**
Faith is the thing you did not go and get, released rather than aimed, by the person nobody sent for,
while afraid.

**THE SECOND TRUE THING**
The giant is made of what the city threw away **and** what it quietly piped out of sight. It was made
by a thousand small carelessnesses, and it is **not** unmade by a thousand small labours. It is
unmade by one open hand. Then the city is given the work of carrying away what it can no longer
pretend it did not make — and that work is gratitude, not payment.

**THE RULE — one sentence, stated once, rehearsed in node 1, obeyed in node 5**

> **Rot is the glue: mixed waste binds because the wet welds it, and anything washed and dried by
> hand carries none of that, so the mass cannot bind through it and comes apart along its own
> materials.**

Force only feeds it. The intake seam is the one place plating can never cover, because it must open
to feed. **And a clean thing thrown is refused; a clean thing given is taken.** That distinction is
the whole film and it is delivered entirely as physics.

**STRUCTURAL DEVIATION (upheld twice).** Node 3 is *the city's* attempt, not the protagonist's. His
exclusion is the engine. His one attempt begins at 2:00.

> ### STANDING PRODUCTION NOTE — binding on gates 3 through 8
>
> **RELIGIOUS VETO.** No religious building, shrine, iconography, statuary, garland, festival
> decoration or painted religious symbol in any frame. Never prompt "Chennai skyline" — prompt named
> buildings only. **Fort St George appears only as the flagstaff and the ramparts; St Mary's Church
> stands inside the fort and no tower or spire may appear.** Marina frames face the sea: sand, surf
> and lighthouse only, promenade and its memorial statuary behind camera. Silk-shop and street
> hoardings are **typography, textile and flat pattern only — no figures, no faces.** The giant is
> built from a **closed component list**: corrugated sheet metal, tarpaulin, fishing net, cable,
> tyres, plastic drums, crates, bicycle frames, cooking pots, sandals, hoarding vinyl, PET bottles,
> cardboard. Nothing else may be embedded in it.
>
> **THE GIANT.** Sixty metres. **Taller than the tallest flagstaff in Madras** — use that relation
> for scale, not numbers. Rounded forward shoulders, head hung, long arms, blunt round hands, tyres
> for knuckles. No claws, spines, horns or spikes. Reference shape: a loaded porter. It sags and
> sheds constantly. Readable component objects at every scale — texture, not grime; no sludge, no
> slime. **The headlamp eyes are unlit, cracked, and reflect the sky. The bus-grille jaw never opens,
> in any node, ever.** One stride every three seconds.
>
> **THE INTAKE SEAM.** Never called a mouth, maw or throat. **Intake seam:** horizontal, shallow,
> letterbox-flat, like a bus radiator grille. Dry clean-edged sheet metal. Backlit pale, so light
> passes through and it reads as an opening to sky. Its motion is breath — slow, steady, tired. Not
> hunger.
>
> **THE COOUM.** Deep opaque olive-green, dull sheen, mats of water hyacinth. **Never black, never
> tar.** Node 6's clean water is the same hue clarifying.
>
> **THE CLEAN LIGHT (node 6).** Not a weapon, not a beam, not a shockwave. It travels outward from
> the seam that received something, unhurried, and **nobody in frame is aiming it or reacting to
> having caused it.** It moves at exactly the speed of a walking crowd. At 2:48 the crowd **stops
> walking and watches — standing, ordinary, some still moving.** No kneeling, no raised hands, no
> tableau.
>
> **LANDMARKS, by their distinguishing features.** Marina — 300+ metres of flat sand between road and
> water. Chennai lighthouse — a modern **triangular tapering concrete tower, red-and-white banded**,
> viewing gallery near the top; not a white cylinder. Napier Bridge — low and long, six
> **black-and-white chequerboard** bowstring arches. Anna flyover — a long sweeping curve of elevated
> road, 500 m, two lanes. Ranganathan Street — extremely narrow, four to six storeys pressed close,
> giant vertical fabric hoardings, cable tangle, only a thin slot of sky. Kuppai medu — flat-topped
> hills of compacted refuse, kite birds, heat haze. *(Tamil: குப்பை மேடு; plural **kuppai medugal**.
> Never "kuppa medus.")*
>
> **PROPS AND LIVERY.** Five-bin handcart: cobalt blue / zinc grey / bottle green / bone white /
> **warm dark-brown** — **identified by colour only, no lettering on the lids.** Question-mark pouch:
> one simple glyph, keep it. MTC ordinary bus brown-and-cream. Chennai auto-rickshaw: yellow body,
> black canopy trim and chassis. **The only legible text in the film is the SEGREGATE AT SOURCE
> hoarding at 0:12 and 2:56 — it is load-bearing and may need plate treatment rather than
> generation.**
>
> **LOCKED AND NOT TO BE ALTERED AT ANY LATER GATE.** Rajan is never given a line explaining why his
> method will work. **"Every round you fire, it —" stays truncated; the second half is never
> restored.** Node 1 and the coda are written in strict order of reveal and must be storyboarded in

>
> **GATES 6–8.** David carried five stones and threw **one** (1 Sam 17:49–50). No title card,
> synopsis or thumbnail copy may imply otherwise. Safe framing: *"he chose five, and needed the one
> nobody would have picked."* And no copy anywhere may state that Chennai lacks waste regulations —
> the rules exist; what lags is enforcement.

EXT. MARINA BEACH - PRE-DAWN

0:00-0:04 —

-- "THE FIRST WRAPPER" --

A mango-yellow foil wrapper, turning slow against flat grey water. The
only saturated colour anywhere.

It cartwheels. Wet sand comes up under it, and then the whole beach --
three hundred metres of flat sand, the lighthouse standing off to the
south, a tide line running with litter. The wrapper stops.

Behind it, small and going away: KAVI, 9, running on. His hand is
still open from letting it go. He does not look back. He does not
think about it at all.

0:04-0:08 —

-- "THE LITTLE KNOT" --

Something comes across the sand toward it. A knot of dry pressed
litter the size of a football -- paper, film, foil, packed tight, no
face, nothing like a face -- hopping and trundling like a paper
hedgehog with somewhere to be.

It goes over the wrapper. The wrapper is gone. The knot is one wrapper
bigger, and it trundles on, taking a straw, taking a bottle cap,
growing as it goes.

0:08-0:16 —

-- "THE PICKER" --

A long-handled picker comes down and takes the wrapper straight back
out of it. The knot stops, put out.

RAJAN straightens up. Corporation greens, sleeves rolled, reflective
striping catching what light there is, glasses fogging at the edges.
Behind him a five-bin handcart -- cobalt, zinc, bottle green, bone
white, dark brown -- with a small wash-jet hose coiled on the side.

And behind that, on the seawall: a Corporation hoarding, sun-bleached
almost to nothing. SEGREGATE AT SOURCE. Its bottom third is buried in
drifted wrappers.

Rajan turns the wrapper over in his fingers. Foil one side, plastic the
other. A thing that belongs to none of the five. He drops it into the
canvas pouch at his hip, the flap marked with a single question mark.

Two joggers pass through him like he is weather.

0:16-0:24 —

-- "THE RINSED BOTTLE" --

The knot is already moving off, fatter than it was.

Rajan takes a clear plastic bottle out of the blue bin, holds it under
the wash-jet until the water running off it is clean, dries it on his
sleeve, and drops it into the knot.

Nothing happens.

He waits. He is not certain. He watches it.

Then the knot shudders -- and comes apart. Not violently. It RESOLVES,
the way a knot resolves when you find the right thread, unspooling
into five neat rills across the sand. Plastic. Metal. Glass. Paper.
And a last small heap of dark garden earth.

Rajan sweeps the five into the five bins with a hand brush. He is
thinking about breakfast.

0:24-0:30 —

-- "THE FIRST TREMOR" --

Far out past the surf, the water shivers in a wide ring. Every gull on
the beach goes up at once.

Rajan is bent over a bottle cap and does not turn around.

1:10-1:16 —

The giant fills the far end of Usman Road. Meera drops her hand.

The street goes white. No muzzles, no projectiles -- just the wash,
and Rajan's face in it, strobing.

And on the giant's plating, the impacts arriving as soft bright
blooms that do not scatter and do not break anything. They SINK IN.
They settle. They tile themselves flat across its chest in overlapping
rows, spreading outward like frost taking a window.

1:16-1:20 —

-- "THE CASING IN HIS HAND" --

A spent casing bounces off the tarmac and rolls to Rajan's feet.

He crouches, because of course he does. He picks it up.

It is tacky. A scrap of foil from the gutter has already bonded itself
to the side of it and will not come off when he pulls.

He looks up at the white light with the casing in his hand. He knows
before anybody in this road knows, and there is not one person here
who will hold still long enough to be told.

1:20-1:30 —

-- "IT KEEPS" --

The firing stops.

The giant is BIGGER. It is wearing a bright new coat of scale-mail
made entirely out of what was shot at it, seam upon seam.

And not one round has touched the intake seam low on its chest.
Nothing ever will. It is the one place plating cannot cover, and the
plating everywhere else is four inches thicker than it was a minute
ago.

The giant reaches one arm down into the mouth of Ranganathan Street --
far too narrow for the rest of it -- and takes the silk-shop awnings
off the fronts, one long draw, and puts them in.

Then it turns and walks back toward the river.

TWO -- a flattened steel tumbler.
