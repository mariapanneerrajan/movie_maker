### COLOUR SCRIPT

```
Node 1  MARINA, FIRST LIGHT (sun not yet above the sea)
        Slate blue, wet-sand grey, seafoam white, Corporation green.
        Desaturated to ~40%. ONE saturated object in the entire node:
        the mango-yellow foil wrapper. That discipline is engineered at
        0:00 and cashed at 2:28.

Node 2  COOUM MOUTH, SUNRISE
        The sun breaks. Highest contrast node in the film. Pale gold rim
        on the giant's east flank, deep blue-cyan in its shadow side and
        on the water. Awe, not fear. The sun is a clean pale gold disc,
        NEVER blood-orange.

Node 3  USMAN ROAD, MID-MORNING, NO SUN
        A hoarding canyon: no direct key, only a thin bright slot of sky
        overhead. Flat, cyan-shifted shade, dust in the air, the
        hoardings' saturated colours knocked back two stops. Then the
        weapons wash the street white — the ONLY hot light in the node,
        and it arrives from the wrong source. The one time this film is
        brightly lit is the one time the city is most wrong. Then flat
        again.

Node 4  COOUM BANK, MID-MORNING, HAZED
        The floor. Lowest saturation in the film — mud, olive, steel,
        wet greens, near-monochrome. The sun is behind the giant's bulk
        and we are in its shadow the whole node. Colour drains HERE, not
        in node 3.

Node 5  THE COOUM — CONTINUOUS. THE HAZE THINS.
        Warmth does not arrive because the sun moves; it is mid-morning
        and the sun stays where it is. It arrives because the HAZE LAYER
        THINS and the disc hardens. A shaft comes through the arches of
        Napier Bridge onto the water where he wades. Warmth lands on HIS
        HANDS and on the five washed objects — not on the giant. Those
        objects are the first clean colours in twenty seconds: clear,
        steel, bottle green, bone white, and the yellow.

Node 6  THE HAZE LIFTS OFF, THEN MIDDAY, THEN BLUE RESTORED
        The haze layer lifts DURING the sorting — motivated: the sorting
        is what lets the light in. 2:30–2:53 is the film's only
        full-spectrum passage. Then a MIDDAY insert for the clean Cooum
        (weeks, months later) — the light state is what announces the
        time jump, not a caption. Then the coda drops back to node 1's
        exact pre-dawn cool blue, unchanged, with the grime gone.
```

**Three distinct light states inside node 6 — mid-morning gold, midday, pre-dawn — must be stated
explicitly in the node-6 prompt or they read as an error.**

### LIGHTING DESIGN

```
Low east sun off the Bay of Bengal is the film's only key light, in
every node but 3. Single-source, deep falloff, no fill beyond sky
bounce off wet sand and water. Node 3 has no key — overhead slot-light
only, plus one wrong-source white wash from off-screen.

The giant is lit by the SAME daylight as everything else. Nothing about
its lighting is special-cased. No underlighting, no rim-in-darkness, no
fire, ever, anywhere in this film.

Marina faces east; the Cooum meets the sea at Marina's north end. So in
nodes 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6 the key comes from THE EAST, LOW, OFF THE SEA.
One restatable direction for five of six nodes.
```

### CAMERA RULE

```
THE CAMERA MOVES ONLY WHEN SOMEONE DECIDES SOMETHING. There are FIVE
moves in 180 seconds. Four of them are Rajan's. The fifth is the city's,
and that transfer is the structure of node 6.

  M1 · 0:18  slow 30 cm push-in on the knot while nothing happens
             (he decides to wait, and is not certain)
  M2 · 1:47  slow 1 m push-in as he steps out of the exo-frame
             (he decides he cannot work in it)
  M3 · 2:01  slow lateral track alongside him past twenty open hatches
             (he decides to take none of it)
  M4 · 2:28  very slow 20 cm rise on the open hand
             (he decides not to throw)
  M5 · 2:46  slow 5.2-second push-in up the Anna flyover deck
             (the city decides — and it is the longest shot in the film)

EVERY OTHER SHOT IN THE FILM IS LOCKED and the world moves through it.
This is also the safest possible Seedance spec: a locked camera with a
moving world renders far more reliably than a moving camera, and it
means the five moves all land.

Corollary, binding on every giant shot: PUT THE SHAKE IN THE WORLD, NOT
THE CAMERA. Dust dropping off a railing, water ringing, a hanging cable
swinging. Never a handheld shudder.
```

*(One permitted exception, marked in place: the settling pan at 2:35.5. It is a settling pan, not a
move of decision, and it may be shot locked if gate 5 prefers.)*

### SCALE BLOCK — paste into every giant shot, verbatim

```
Camera height 1.6 m, level. The camera never drifts up toward the
giant's head. Horizon on the lower third line. A DEPTH LADDER of at
least two known-size objects between camera and giant: near (a railing,
a lamp post, an auto-rickshaw), mid (an MTC bus, a four-storey
shopfront), far (the giant). AERIAL PERSPECTIVE UP THE BODY: haze
increases with height; the head reads two stops flatter and bluer than
the feet. ONE ARCHITECTURAL COMPARISON, stated as a relation, never as
a number — the giant is TALLER THAN THE FORT ST GEORGE FLAGSTAFF, which
is the tallest thing standing in Madras. TEMPO: one stride every three
seconds, no fast limb motion, a third of the speed you would expect.
```

### SHOT COUNT AND TEMPO

**119 shots in 180 seconds.** Average 1.5s. Per node: **N1 15 · N2 18 · N3 19 · N4 18 · N5 25 · N6 24.**

Almost every shot is a *locked frame with one event in it* — montage grammar, which is what a
three-minute film carrying this much story needs. The breath is engineered in specific places: the 2.4s
frost tile, the 2.6s resolve, the 2.0s empty pouch, the 1.8s open hand, and the **5.2s sustained
flyover push-in, the longest shot in the film by a factor of two, and the one where nobody does
anything.**

**Node 5 (25 shots) and node 6 (24 shots) are the risk.** If Seedance will not hold the count, merge in
