# 00 — Ideas

## Seed

"I want to create a movie to share how even an underdog can succeed. I want to show the
story of David versus Goliath in the movie itself. I want to capture the key scene where
David believes in God and goes and uses the pebble to strike the giant and make it fall.
I want this story to be told in a sci-fi setting. I want a lot of very interesting shots.
It should be highly action-oriented, with a lot of visual epic camera shots. I want to
have slow-motion shots and 360 shots that emphasize the impact of the storytelling. I
wanna convey the importance of faith and how, even as an underdog, even when things don't
look like it's quite going your way, by having faith, you can overcome. You can do the
impossible. I want the audience to feel empowered, inspired, and motivated to believe in
themselves that anything is possible if they believe."

Distilled: David vs Goliath (1 Samuel 17), sci-fi setting, highly action-oriented, epic
camera work (slow-motion, 360°). Feeling to transfer: empowered, inspired, motivated —
faith makes the impossible possible.

## Set 1

1. An orbital shepherd, his luminous flock of sail-drifters about to be swallowed by a
   kilometre-long harvester hulk, releases one ball bearing into the dark — then turns his
   back on it.
   Anchor: 1 Samuel 17:34-36 — the shepherd who fought lions for the flock before he ever faced
   a giant; joined to the single stone of v.49. The turn carries "the battle is not mine": he
   lets it go and stops watching. Faith rendered as a man who does not need to see the shot land.
   Why it works: zero-g gives 360s and slow-motion for free; the bearing drifts away looking
   pathetic, Rajan faces the hulk empty-handed with the sail-flock glowing behind him — and the
   bearing comes back around the whole world from behind, at orbital speed, through the one
   unshielded sensor eye.

2. On an ash plain a mirrored siege-titan walks through an army's massed fire untouched; Rajan
   steps out alone with a woven cable sling, and the giant lifts its visor to roar at him.
   Anchor: 1 Samuel 17:5-7 and 17:49 — the armour catalogued piece by piece, complete except the
   face; the champion's own boast opens the only gap. The taunt is the wound.
   Why it works: the most spectacular version of what Rajan asked for — the whirl in slow motion,
   the 360 around a lone man in front of a hundred-metre hull, and the turn is that the titan's
   own contempt opens its visor a half-second before the stone arrives.

3. A sky-farmer on a gas giant watches a kilometres-tall lightning-titan grind toward the last
   living fields; he slings one glass seed into its heart, and the giant comes down as rain.
   Anchor: 1 Samuel 17:52-53 — the giant's fall is not an ending but a harvest; the whole nation
   eats because one man stood. Deliverance that turns into provision for people who did nothing
   to earn it.
   Why it works: the destroyer collapsing into a warm downpour across cracked terraces is an
   image nobody has seen, and the turn is tonal — terror becomes water, and Rajan stands in it
   with his face up while the fields go green under him.

4. On a scrapworld, Rajan pushes away a borrowed war-rig, rides his own battered skiff straight
   at the harvester-crawler eating his settlement, and whips one magnetic slug into its drive-coil.
   Anchor: 1 Samuel 17:38-39 ("I cannot walk in these, I have not tested them") joined to 17:51,
   where the giant is finished with the giant's own sword. Borrowed strength fails; the enemy's
   own power undoes it.
   Why it works: playful, kinetic chase energy under the crawler's treads, and the turn is
   visible in one shot — the slug latches, the crawler's own engine surges, and the machine tears
   itself open while Rajan doesn't even look back.

5. A colossus that grows to match whatever is thrown at it has fed on an army's artillery until
   it blots out the sky; Rajan walks out and flicks one small stone at it.
   Anchor: 1 Samuel 17:45,47 — "not by sword or spear." The fear you arm against grows to the
   size of the weapon you bring it; the thing that ends it is the thing that refuses the contest.
   Why it works: the strangest and best turn on the list — the mountain-sized figure convulses,
   folds inward, and shrinks down and down to match its challenger until it is a pebble in the
   dust, and Rajan picks it up and pockets it.

Scout's recommendation: #1, the orbital shepherd — the camera language Rajan wants (true
360, long slow-motion drift) is what that world already does, and the turn is the exact
feeling he named.

## Set 2

Direction for this set (Rajan, verbatim): "give me five more ideas. I wanted it to be a
very simple idea. It should be epic and fantastic to watch, but at the same time, it
should be very simple to understand. I want a very simple story that old people and even
really young people will be able to understand and enjoy"

1. A colossal walking war-machine strides toward a colony wall packed with hiding families;
   Rajan steps out alone onto the chrome sand, whirls a sling, and one stone drops it.
   Anchor: 1 Samuel 17:8-11 and 17:32 — the whole army frozen in fear while one man steps out
   from behind the line. The pattern is a champion who stands between the giant and the people,
   carried as a lone silhouette in front of a wall of faces.
   Why it works: the purest version of what Rajan asked for — 360 around the whirl, the stone in
   slow motion crossing a hundred metres of desert, the machine coming down like a falling tower
   and the dust wave washing over the cheering wall.

2. On a white ice plain, a plated colossus the size of a cathedral charges a lone figure; Rajan
   does not run, and one glowing stone brings it down sliding to a stop at his boots.
   Anchor: 1 Samuel 17:34-37 — the shepherd who had already fought the lion and the bear in
   private, so the giant was simply the next one. Courage that was built where nobody watched.
   Why it works: creature weight in the Jurassic Park register — spray of ice, breath fogging,
   the ground hammering — and the whole turn is that he plants his feet instead of running.

3. A man stands on a bare asteroid while a warship the size of a moon fills the entire sky above
   him; he throws one stone, and the moon-sized ship falls burning past the planet below.
   Anchor: 1 Samuel 17:45-47 — "not by sword or spear," and the scale of it made public so that
   everyone watching knows the small one did not do it alone.
   Why it works: no world-building needed at all — one glance tells a five-year-old everything.
   Tiny man, sky-filling ship, one thrown pebble with a comet-trail, and the slowest, largest
   fall the format can hold.

4. A giant of churning black smoke and embers wades through a night ocean, swallowing the lights
   of a floating city; Rajan throws one small glowing seed into its chest, and it bursts into
   light and falls.
   Anchor: 1 Samuel 17:49 laid over the pattern of a light that the dark cannot put out — the
   smallest lit thing being enough, and the fall of the enemy giving the light back to everyone.
   Why it works: light beats dark is the one story every age already knows, and the climax is the
   most beautiful image on this list — a hundred-metre darkness lighting up from the inside and
   coming apart into sparks that drift down over a relit city.

5. Rajan walks past a mountain of shattered weapons left by everyone who tried, faces an armoured
   giant in a red canyon, throws one of his five stones, and opens his palm to show four left.
   Anchor: 1 Samuel 17:40 — five smooth stones chosen from the brook, and only one ever needed.
   The pile of broken giant-weapons is 17:24, the army that ran, made visible in one image.
   Why it works: swagger, and a closing image a child reads instantly — four stones still resting
   in an open hand while the giant lies in the dust behind him.

Scout's recommendation: #3, the man on the asteroid — the scale contrast tells the entire
story in the first frame; nothing needs explaining.

## Set 3

Direction for this set (Rajan, verbatim): "give me five more ideas. Keep it simple, and I
want a lot of action sequences in this movie. I wanted it to be in an arena. Maybe what
if we just keep it on another planet where the hero is fighting against this giant
monster creature maybe in the beginning, it looks a bit uninformative, and let's not just
use a pebble that gets thrown. Instead, what if we have an actual fight sequence where
the man is fighting against this massive giant? Maybe in the beginning, the man has
weapons, and he fights, but then the monster just thrashes, breaks his weapons, throws it
down, thrashes him, and throws him off. He's on the ground, and then he just finds a
pebble lying there in the arena. He takes his scarf, puts the pebbles inside, and slings
it at the giant, and it just falls down. Give me some ideas on these lines. Keep it very
simple because the movie is just 30 seconds."

Fixed spine for this set: arena on another planet · Rajan vs giant monster creature ·
real fight with weapons first · weapons broken, hero thrashed down · pebble found in the
arena · scarf becomes the sling · one slung stone drops the giant.

1. In a crater arena of black glass under twin suns, Rajan's plasma glaives shatter against a
   horned saurian titan; beaten in the dust, he loads a shard of arena glass into his scarf.
   Anchor: 1 Samuel 17:38-39 — the borrowed armour and the king's sword that fail, versus the
   thing the shepherd already had on him. The arena hands him its finest weapons; what saves him
   is what he walked in wearing.
   Why it works: the loudest, most classic version — a roaring alien crowd, 360 around the whirling
   scarf, and the titan coming down so hard the dust wave rolls up over the tiers and silences
   every one of them.

2. On a ring of floating stone platforms above a chasm, a crystal-plated serpent-beast snaps
   Rajan's spear and hurls him to the lowest slab, where one dark pebble waits in the rubble.
   Anchor: 1 Samuel 17:47 — "the battle is not yours." Every solid thing he stands on gives way
   until he is at the bottom with nothing, which is exactly where the win comes from.
   Why it works: vertigo and falling debris give the fight real kinetic danger, and the finish is
   the beast toppling backward off the last platform into the chasm, its plates going dark one
   by one as it drops out of frame.

3. In a rain-flooded arena, ankle-deep water exploding around a horned ape-saurian, Rajan's axes
   are torn away — face down in the shallows, his fingers close on a smooth stone.
   Anchor: 1 Samuel 17:40 — five smooth stones taken out of the brook. He is knocked into the
   water and the water is what arms him; the place of his lowest moment is the supply.
   Why it works: water is the best material slow-motion has — the soaked scarf whirling in a
   360 throwing spiral arcs of spray, and the beast's fall blasting a full ring of water outward
   across the arena into the torchlight.

4. On a white ice floor under a shattered moon, silent hooded spectators watch a prism-plated
   colossus snap Rajan's blade — then one dark stone, the only dark thing there, catches his eye.
   Anchor: 1 Samuel 17:24-26 — an entire army that watched and did nothing, and one man who
   asked why nobody was standing. The hush is the point; the crowd never helps.
   Why it works: the cleanest image on the list — red scarf, dark stone, white world — and the
   colossus fracturing from the inside with light pouring out through the cracks as it comes down
   and floods the ice with colour.

5. Inside an arena built in the ribcage of something long dead, a magma-veined stone beast breaks
   Rajan's hammer and throws him down among cooled black pebbles.
   Anchor: 1 Samuel 17:51-52 — the giant falls and a whole people who had been pinned in place
   get up. The bones of a bigger giant already in the ground say the same thing without a word.
   Why it works: the strangest arena of the five, and the finish is the glow dying seam by seam
   through the beast as it falls, the arena going dark, and the ragged tiers rising to their feet
   in the last two seconds.

Scout's recommendation: #3, the rain-flooded arena — the pebble comes out of water as in
the passage, and rain gives the slow-motion and 360 work something spectacular to move
through.

## Chosen

Set 3, idea 5 — the ribcage arena, with Rajan's modifications (verbatim): "I like the rib
cage arena. Make sure it is happening in daylight, where everything is well lit and
clearly visible. I want the setting to be like a jungle. I don't want any other crowd or
characters. This should be like a luscious jungle inside which we see this rib cage arena
where this stone beast is fighting with Rajan, also, let's have Rajan dressed up as a
cave man."

Final logline: Inside a colossal ribcage arena standing in a lush daylight jungle, a
magma-veined stone beast breaks caveman Rajan's hammer and throws him down among cooled
black pebbles — until one stone, slung from his scarf, brings the giant down. No crowd,
no other characters.
