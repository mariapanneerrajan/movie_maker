## FINDINGS

### 1. [BLOCKING] Node 6, 2:51–2:55 "THE RIVER CLEARS" — and the film's whole causal spine

**CLAIM:** "MARINA BEACH: a line of people the whole width of the sand... Five colours of sack,
everywhere, all at once." → "The Cooum, from the same spot at Napier Bridge, later — weeks, months.
Green water. Green banks."

**FINDING:** The film's chain is litter → giant → citizens pick up litter and segregate → the Cooum
runs clear. **That is a false statement about the mechanism of a real river's pollution.** The Cooum
is black because of **untreated sewage**, not solid-waste littering. The CRRT's initial enumeration
found 118 sewage outfalls into the Cooum, with roughly 900 more still to be plugged; nearly 70,000
families along its course drain illegal sewage inlets into it; Wikipedia gives 127 identified
outfalls, 85 in use, taking ~30% of Chennai's 55 MLD of untreated sewage. CPCB's 2022 report named
the Cooum the most polluted river in India. No amount of citizen litter-picking cleans it;
interception & diversion works and sewage treatment plants do. Presenting the litter fix as
sufficient misrepresents a live public problem and misdirects the audience the film explicitly wants
to implicate.

**SOURCES:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooum_River · citizenmatters.in/reviving-the-cooum-need-for-
innovation-enforcement-and-shared-responsibility · groundreport.in/environment/restoring-chennais-
dead-cooum-river-faces-ongoing-challenges-4753805

**FIX:** Additive, not a rewrite — two seconds of screen time. In the 2:47–2:51 montage or the 2:51
river beat, add one image of the other half of the job: **an open outfall pipe on the bank being
capped and a new aeration line turning in the channel**, with workers on it, while the citizens work
the banks. The film then says truthfully: the city picked up what it dropped *and* stopped what it
was piping in. It strengthens the theme (the giant was made of what was thrown away *and* what was
quietly let out of sight), and it keeps the conservancy workers and the Corporation as part of the
rescue rather than as the absent party.

### 2. [BLOCKING] Node 3, 1:00–1:22 — Ranganathan Street cannot hold this scene

**CLAIM:** "Twenty exo-frames fill the street mouth, shoulder to shoulder — three metres each" and
"The giant turns into the top of Ranganathan Street, and it barely fits."

**FINDING:** Ranganathan Street's carriageway is about **7 metres** wide; encroachment reduces the
nominal 30-foot road to as little as 10 feet in places. Twenty three-metre frames shoulder to
shoulder need ~60 m of frontage — nine times the street's width. And a sixty-metre giant has
shoulders roughly 20–25 m across and a foot 8–10 m long; it does not "barely fit" into a 7 m lane,
**it cannot enter it at all.** The draft's own next line ("Silk-shop hoardings four storeys tall")
confirms a canyon, not a boulevard.

**SOURCES:** grokipedia.com/page/Ranganathan_Street · spiritofchennai.com/smartcity/tnagar-
pedestrian-plaza-2 · en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranganathan_Street

**FIX:** Two changes, both of which make the beat better. **(a)** Stage the armoured line on **Usman
Road** — the wide arterial Ranganathan Street runs off — with Ranganathan Street as the narrow slot
behind them the crowd has emptied down. **(b)** The giant does not enter Ranganathan Street; it
**fills the far end of Usman Road and reaches one arm down into the slot** to take the silk-shop
awnings, which is a far more frightening image than a giant strolling a shopping lane. If the room
wants to keep the line inside Ranganathan Street, reduce it to **four frames abreast and sixteen
ranked behind** — a canyon of armour in a canyon of hoardings, which is the truer and more cinematic
picture of that street anyway.

### 3. [STRONG] Node 3, 1:00 — "the narrowest, busiest street in the city"

**FINDING:** "Busiest" is well supported — routinely called the most crowded street in Chennai and
one of the busiest shopping streets in Asia. **"Narrowest" is not true** and is not a claim any
source makes; Chennai has far narrower lanes in Georgetown, Triplicane and Mylapore.

**FIX:** "The most crowded street in the city, and it is empty." Same rhythm, and the emptiness
lands harder against "most crowded" than against "narrowest."

### 4. [STRONG] Node 2, 0:57–1:00 — the kuppa medu is not fifteen kilometres north

**FINDING:** Chennai has two dump yards and neither matches. **Kodungaiyur** is north, ~350 acres
(GCC's own page says 269 acres, 35+ years, 2,600–2,800 MT/day), roughly **10 km** from Chennai Beach

Marina shoreline.

**SOURCES:** alldistancebetween.com (Kodungaiyur–Beach Station, 10.00 km) · theprint.in ground
report on Perungudi (250 acres, ~3,000 MT/day, ~20 km from Marina) · chennaicorporation.gov.in SWM
department page (6,150 MT/day citywide)

**FIX:** "Ten kilometres north, on the **Kodungaiyur** skyline, one of the garbage hills stands up
too." Naming it costs one word and buys enormous local credibility. Bonus for node 6: the second
giant being unbuilt in the north *and* the south — Perungudi is the mirror image, which quietly
doubles the "whole city" image the ending wants.

### 5. [STRONG] Standing note + nodes 2 and 6 — "kuppa medu" is the wrong transliteration

**FINDING:** The Tamil is **குப்பை மேடு**, transliterated **kuppai medu** — குப்பை (*kuppai*) =
garbage/rubbish, மேடு (*mēdu*) = mound/high ground. "Kuppa" drops the final vowel and is not the
Tamil word. Plural is **kuppai medugal** (குப்பை மேடுகள்).

**SOURCES:** en.wiktionary.org/wiki/குப்பை · shabdkosh.com Tamil–English

**FIX:** Correct to **kuppai medu** / **kuppai medugal** everywhere, including any downstream asset
name. The seed spells it "Kuppa Medus" in Rajan's own words, so confirm with him before overriding —
but the Tamil is unambiguous, and *medus* with an English plural should not survive into a title
card.

### 6. [STRONG] Node 2, 0:52 — Anna Salai and the Anna flyover treated as two different places

**FINDING:** The Anna Flyover (Gemini flyover) **is** Anna Salai — it carries the Anna Salai
carriageway over the Nungambakkam High Road / Cathedral Road junction at Gemini Circle. 500 m long,
2 lanes, max 20 m wide, opened 1 July 1973, Chennai's first flyover and India's third. As written
the line reads as though the giant crosses one road to reach a different one.

**SOURCE:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Flyover

**FIX:** "The giant steps over **Cathedral Road** and puts one foot down on the Anna flyover." (Or
"comes up Anna Salai and puts one foot down on the Anna flyover.") The flyover is only 500 m and two
lanes — "strides along the flyover as though it were a kerb" is three strides at most, which is
fine, but the storyboard should not plan a long march. Also at 0:52 the draft says hoardings drift
"down over the traffic" four lines after establishing "Cars have been abandoned mid-lane with their
doors open" — change to "the abandoned lanes."

### 7. [STRONG] Beat sheet "INVENTED PHYSICS" vs Node 5 — the rule and the fifth object contradict

**FINDING:** Internal, not real-world, but it is the load-bearing beam of the film. The rule requires
the object to be *sorted*. The fifth stone is by definition **unsortable** — that is its entire
identity, and the beat sheet leans on Psalm 118:22 to make the point. The film has two competing
mechanisms and states neither cleanly, and node 1's demonstration uses the *other* kind of object (a
rinsed, perfectly sortable bottle), so the audience is trained on rule A and paid off on rule B.

**FIX:** Pick one, and make node 1 rehearse it.
- **Option A (cleanest):** drop "sorted" from the rule. "A thing that has been cleaned and dried
  carries no rot and cannot be bonded to." The wrapper works because it is clean and dry, and its
  unbinnability stays as pure theme — the man who has no bin for it is the man nobody has a use for.
  Node 1 stands as written.
- **Option B (stronger, more work):** make unsortability the mechanism. A thing that belongs to no
  stream cannot join any stream; the mass has to *decide* where to put it, and deciding is sorting.
  Node 1's little knot must then be resolved by **a wrapper from his pouch**, not a bottle, and the
  pouch becomes the film's whole engine from 0:22 onward.

Either way the rule must be stated once, in one sentence, and obeyed twice.

### 8. [STRONG] Node 5, 2:20–2:30 — why the fifth works is not legible on screen

**FINDING:** Node 3 correctly plants the target ("Not one round has touched the breathing seam... It
is the one place plating cannot cover"). But node 5 never shows Arul *aiming* at it. As written,
four throws read as random flailing and the fifth reads as **luck** — which is the one reading that
kills the film, because luck is not faith and it is not cleverness either. The audience must see
that he has a target, that it is fifty metres above his head, and that a man cannot throw that far.
Then the contrary act — stop throwing, stand under the intake, open your hand, and let the giant's
own breath carry it — becomes the David inversion the logline promises.

**FIX:** Three words per throw. "He throws the bottle **up at the seam**. Short — it strikes the new
plating and spins away. The tumbler, **higher**. Deflected off a tyre knuckle. The jar, **everything
he has**. Gone into the netting, ten metres under the seam. The newspaper block barely leaves his
hand." Then at 2:27 he looks at his last object, looks up at the seam, and does not throw.

### 9. [STRONG] Nodes 1, 5 and 6 — the pouch already contains a fifth stone

**FINDING:** Arul has been carrying an identical clean dry unbinnable foil wrapper on his hip since
0:12, and the film makes a point of him confirming it is still there at 2:06 — and then he goes
fishing in black water for the same object. A viewer paying attention (and the film asks them to pay
attention to that pouch — it gets its own titled beat) will ask why. It also muddies the closing
image, because we cannot tell whether the wrapper Kavi drops into the pouch at 2:58 is a replacement
for one that was spent or the same one that was never used.

**FIX:** Two clean options.
- **(a) Empty the pouch.** At 2:06 he reaches for it and the flap is hanging open — the Cooum took
  it. *Then* he goes in for five, and the river gives one back. This makes the fifth stone a gift
  rather than an inventory item, which is what "Help me find them" is asking for anyway.
- **(b) Make the pouch the fifth stone.** He brings four out of the water, sets them in the row, then
  unbuttons the pouch for the fifth. Five stones from the brook still holds if the fifth is the one
  he has carried for twenty years because nobody would take it. Thematically stronger — literally
  Psalm 118:22 — but it costs the river-gives-it-back beat.

### 11. [SUGGESTION] Node 2, 0:30–0:40 — the Chennai Central sightline is doubtful

**FINDING:** Everything about the station is right — it is on the Cooum's course, its 136-foot clock
tower is the signature feature, renamed Puratchi Thalaivar Dr. M.G. Ramachandran Central Railway
Station on 5 April 2019 (both "MGR Chennai Central" and colloquial "Central" are fine). What could
not be verified is the **sightline**. Central is roughly 2.2 km north-west of Napier Bridge, and a
~41 m tower at that distance sits behind the Fort St George complex, the Island Grounds tree line
and the Ripon Building block. No source, photograph or description places Central's tower in the
view from Napier Bridge. **I am not willing to certify it.**

**FIX:** Three safe routes. **(a)** Use a landmark demonstrably right there: the **Fort St George
flagstaff**, 150 feet of teak, the tallest in India, standing immediately north of Napier Bridge and
flown day and night — secular, instantly Chennai, and a far more graphic silhouette than a distant
tower. **(b)** Move the node-2 wide upstream to the Cooum at Chintadripet/Egmore, where Central
genuinely dominates the bank. **(c)** Hedge: "Beyond it, the city — the clock tower somewhere in
it." **Recommend (a)**; it also gives node 6 a flag over a clean river.

### 15. [SUGGESTION] Cast note — "Corporation greens" is unverified, and the true detail is better

**FINDING:** **"Conservancy worker" is exactly right** — the term used in Chennai for this job,
alongside "sanitary worker." The **green** could not be verified. What is documented: workers under
contractors Urbaser Sumeet and Ramky Enviro are issued "two pairs of reflective uniforms, gloves,
masks and shoes," while GCC-employed workers report *not* being given reflective jackets; the TN
Handloom Society supplies GCC's sanitary workers with shirts and pants, caps, towels and bags, no
colour stated. Set ~15 years on, a green Corporation uniform is a costume choice, not a falsehood —
but not a certifiable fact either.

**FIX:** Keep the greens — an invention the film is entitled to — but add the one documented detail
that also solves a cinematography problem: **reflective high-vis striping on sleeves and back.** At
pre-dawn on the Marina that stripe is the only thing that reads at distance, and it gives Arul a
visual signature that survives at sixty metres. Also usable: the real 6:00–6:30 am shift start is
exactly the hour node 1 is set in.

### 16. [SUGGESTION] Node 1 and the seed — do not let "there are no rules" back into the film

**FINDING:** The draft does not state this and is **commended** for it — the film asserts no such
thing; it shows a careless hand, which is honest. The **seed** does state it ("They are not having
strict regulations..."), and it is false, so it needs a guard before gates 6–8. The real picture is
**rules exist; compliance and enforcement are patchy.** Source segregation is mandatory under the
Solid Waste Management Rules (2016, revised and in force from 1 April 2026 — four streams: wet, dry,
sanitary, domestic hazardous). GCC's SWM Bye-Law 2016 sets fines from Rs 100 through Rs 1,000
(residents dumping street waste) to Rs 25,000 (event organisers who litter, burn, bury or fail to
segregate); non-segregating households Rs 1,000. Door-to-door collection covers ~95% of households,
6,150 MT/day. GCC is currently prosecuting bulk waste generators at Rs 5,000–25,000 following a
Supreme Court order of 25 May. **What lags is enforcement, not legislation.**

**FIX:** One image, and it earns its place. In node 1, on the seawall behind Arul, a **faded
Corporation hoarding: "SEGREGATE AT SOURCE" — half-covered in litter.** The rule existed. Nobody
kept it. Accurate, and a sharper indictment than "nobody made a rule" — it converts the charge from
*the city has no law* to *the city has a law it walks past*, which is closer to the fall the charter
is about. Bind it as a note so no downstream copy says Chennai lacks waste regulations.

**FIX:** "**Sixty crore of armour lying in the mud**" — Rs 600 million, about Rs 3 crore

it.

### 18. [SUGGESTION] Node 6, 2:51 — "green water" reads as an algal bloom, not a clean river

**FINDING:** The herons are correct and well grounded — cattle egrets and pond herons are documented

Specifying pond heron or egret also gives the asset stage the right silhouette — small, hunched,

### 19. [SUGGESTION] Node 1, 0:26–0:30 — gulls are seasonal, and the film has already chosen right

**FINDING:** True, with a condition. Brown-headed gulls (with black-headed gulls, terns, little

**FIX:** Add to the standing note: "Season: north-east monsoon, October–December — this is what puts
gulls on the Marina and the blue in the pre-dawn." Specify **brown-headed gulls** for the asset

### 20. [SUGGESTION] Scripture anchor — five stones, one thrown

## VERIFIED CLEAN

- **The Cooum empties into the Bay of Bengal beside Napier Bridge, at the north end of Marina
  Beach** — confirmed. The river reaches the sea at Chepauk, "marking the northernmost boundary of
  the Marina Beach," and merges with the bay south of Fort St George just under Napier Bridge.
- **Napier Bridge is over the Cooum, carries traffic, and has footpaths a child could lean over** —
  confirmed. 6 lanes, 2 m footpaths, 138 m, six bowstring-arch spans, built 1869, rebuilt 1944,
  second carriageway 1999.
- **Marina Beach faces east; the sun rises out of the sea** — confirmed. The film's entire dawn
  scheme, including "somewhere behind the city, a drone lamp is still burning" (i.e. to the west), is
  sound.
- **The lighthouse stands off to the south from the north end of Marina** — confirmed. The beach runs
  "from the Victory War Memorial near the Cooum River delta till the lighthouse to the south," a
  3.1 km stretch; at 46 m tall (light at 57 m) it is comfortably visible down an open beach.
- **Ranganathan Street is in T. Nagar and is the city's busiest** — confirmed; runs Usman Road to
  Mambalam station, lined with textile and jewellery houses, so "silk-shop hoardings four storeys
  tall" is right in character.
- **Chennai's dump yards, their names, and the film's ending** — confirmed, and the ending is more
  accurate than it knows. Kodungaiyur (north) and Perungudi (south), both 35+ years old, and **both
  are currently being biomined and reclaimed** — 1,730,584 m³ processed at Perungudi, 22 lakh tons at
  Kodungaiyur, the Kodungaiyur land planned as an urban forest. Node 6's "hills being taken down and
  rebuilt" is a real programme in progress.
- **Herons in a cleaned Cooum** — confirmed; cattle egrets and pond herons documented in the Cooum
  and Adyar. Node 1's simile ("the way a heron takes a fish") is also sound for this coast.
- **"Conservancy worker" is the right term in Chennai** — confirmed.
- **"Anna" as a child's address to a young man** — confirmed. அண்ணா / அண்ணன் = elder brother, used
  warmly and respectfully for any older male. The draft's gloss is accurate for a 9-year-old
  addressing a man in his late 30s. *Free resonance:* C. N. Annadurai, for whom Anna Salai and the
  Anna flyover are named, was himself called *Anna* — elder brother. The boy on the giant is calling
  Arul by the name the city's main road already carries.
- **MTC is the correct operator for Chennai city buses** — confirmed.
- **"MGR Chennai Central"** — confirmed; renamed 5 April 2019; Victorian-Gothic, 136-foot clock tower.
- **1 Samuel 17:38–40** — confirmed against KJV: "he took his staff in his hand, and chose him five
  smooth stones out of the brook, and put them in a shepherd's bag." Reference, content and context
  all correct.
- **1 Samuel 17:34–37 (the lion and the bear)** — confirmed. The beat sheet's use of it is a sound
  reading, and node 1's football-sized knot is a legitimate dramatisation of it.
- **1 Samuel 17:45 ("not with sword and spear")** — confirmed; the film is on the right side of the
  verse.
- **Psalm 118:22 / Matthew 21:42** — both confirmed against KJV, and Matthew is indeed Jesus quoting
  the Psalm.
- **No Scripture is quoted, paraphrased or spoken on screen** — confirmed by reading every line.
  Anchors live in the header only, exactly as craft-story prescribes.
- **The "no religious buildings" note holds** — confirmed. Every landmark named is secular. No
  temple, mosque, church, shrine, devotional statue or festival image appears anywhere in the text.
  *(Watch item for gates 3–5: Santhome Basilica and the Kapaleeshwarar temple sit close to the

- **Six nodes tile 180 seconds with no gap and no overlap** — confirmed, every internal timestamp
