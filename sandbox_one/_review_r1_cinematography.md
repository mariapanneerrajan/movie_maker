## DELIVERABLE 1 — THE COLOUR SCRIPT AND THE TIME-OF-DAY FIX

The draft's tone line — *"Monsoon-blue pre-dawn, warm sodium night, gold at the end"* — has no night
in it. **There is no night anywhere in this film. Delete "warm sodium night."** The only defensible
sodium in the picture is one drone lamp still burning behind the city at 0:00, and I'd move that out
of the rhyme frame entirely (see Finding 8).

The real journey is **blue → gold → blue restored**, and that is better than blue → gold, because it
lets the last image mean "the ordinary morning, redeemed" instead of "happily ever after."

```
COLOUR SCRIPT

Node 1  MARINA, FIRST LIGHT (sun not yet above the sea)
        Slate blue, wet-sand grey, seafoam white, Corporation green.
        Desaturated to ~40%. ONE saturated object in the entire node:
        the mango-yellow foil wrapper. That discipline is the whole
        film's payoff engineered at 0:03.

Node 2  COOUM MOUTH, SUNRISE
        The sun breaks. Highest contrast node in the film. Pale gold
        rim on the giant's east flank, deep blue-cyan in its shadow
        side and on the water. Awe, not fear — the sun is a clean pale
        gold disc, NEVER blood-orange.

Node 3  RANGANATHAN STREET, MID-MORNING, NO SUN
        A north-south canyon: no direct key, only a thin bright slot
        of sky overhead. Flat, cyan-shifted shade, dust in the air,
        the hoardings' saturated colours knocked back two stops. Then
        the weapons wash the street white — the ONLY hot light in the
        node, and it arrives from the wrong source. The one time this
        film is brightly lit is the one time the city is most wrong.
        Then flat again.

Node 4  COOUM BANK, HAZED MORNING
        The floor. Lowest saturation in the film — mud, olive, steel,
        wet greens, near-monochrome. The sun is behind the giant's
        bulk and we are in its shadow the whole node. Colour drains
        HERE, not in node 3.

Node 5  THE COOUM, LOW EAST SUN RETURNING
        A single shaft of low sun comes through the bridge structure
        onto the water where he wades. Warmth arrives on HIS HANDS and
        on the five washed objects — not on the giant. The objects are
        the first clean colours in twenty seconds: clear, steel, green
        glass, paper-white, and the yellow.

Node 6  FULL GOLD, THEN BLUE RESTORED
        The sun clears the horizon haze DURING the sorting —
        motivated: the sorting is what lets the light in. 2:30-2:51 is
        the film's only full-spectrum passage. Then the coda drops
        back to Node 1's exact cool blue, unchanged, with the grime
        gone.
```

**Time-of-day progression, corrected:** Node 1 first light (pre-sunrise) → Node 2 sunrise → Node 3
mid-morning → Node 4 mid-morning, hazed → Node 5 continuous → Node 6 continuous, then a **midday**
insert for the clean Cooum (weeks later), then back to **first light** for the coda. Three distinct
light states inside node 6 is the clearest way to sell the time jump — but it must be stated
explicitly or it reads as an error.

**One geographic fact that governs all lighting:** Marina faces east; the Cooum mouth is at the north
end of Marina. So in nodes 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6 the key comes from **the east, low, off the sea.** That
is a single, restatable, verbatim direction for four of six nodes. Node 3 is the deliberate exception
— no key at all. That is the lighting design in two sentences.

```
LIGHTING DESIGN
  Low east sun off the Bay of Bengal is the film's only key light, in
  every node but 3. Single-source, deep falloff, no fill beyond sky
  bounce off wet sand and water. Node 3 has no key — overhead
  slot-light only. The giant is lit by the SAME daylight as everything
  else; nothing about its lighting is special-cased. No underlighting,
  no rim-in-darkness, no fire, ever, anywhere.

LENS LANGUAGE
  21mm  — the scale lens. Camera at 1.6m. Giant + known-size
          foreground. Never used on a face.
  35mm  — the honest eye. Marina, the rhyme frames, Arul working,
          giant body parts at human scale.
  85mm  — scarce. Four close-ups in the whole film, plus Kavi. The
          turn lives here.

GRADE / RENDER CHARACTER
  Kodak Vision3 250D, gentle halation on highlights, fine grain,
  blacks that stay blue rather than crushing to true black, painterly
  falloff — a children's picture-book plate lit like a 1980s Amblin
  daylight exterior. No anamorphic flare streaks.

ASPECT RATIO
  2.39:1. Chosen deliberately AGAINST the tall subject: the film is
  horizontal (a wide beach, a long street, a long flyover, five piles
  settling across a pan, a line of people the width of the sand), and
  a 60-metre figure that cannot be contained by the frame is the point.

CAMERA RULE
  The camera moves only when Arul decides something. Everywhere else
  it is locked and the WORLD moves through it. This is also the
  Seedance-safest rule available: a locked camera with a moving world
  renders far more reliably than a moving camera, and it means the
  four or five moves in the film all land.
```

## DELIVERABLE 2 — THE FIVE-COLOUR SPINE (the draft's best unexploited asset)

The draft has, without capitalising on it, built the film's strongest continuity device: **the five
bin lids at 0:12 are the five streams at 2:33 are the five sacks at 2:47 are the five mountains at
2:52.** Lock those five hues verbatim and the six nodes will look like one film even where nothing
else matches.

| Stream | Colour | Locked string |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plastic | Blue | cobalt blue |
| Metal | Grey | zinc grey |
| Glass | Green | bottle green |
| Paper | White | bone white |
| Wet waste | **Warm earth brown** | terracotta brown |

**Critical:** the draft renders wet waste as *"dark and heavy"* — the one stream that can read as
sludge and undo the whole resolution. Give it a warm earth-brown and let it read as **soil**. Then at
2:52 the wet-waste mound already has green shoots on it. That converts the ugliest of the five into
the most hopeful. A two-word change with a large return.

## FINDINGS

### 1. [STRONG · BLOCKER] Node 6, 2:30–2:38 — the sorting will not generate as written

**PROBLEM:** Five simultaneous, materially distinct streams separating out of a collapsing
sixty-metre mass, in one continuous shot. This is a compound instruction; Seedance will average it
into an undifferentiated debris cloud — the exact read (destruction) the shot exists to avoid. Do not
carry this to gate 5 in this form.

**FIX:** Make it **sequential, and separate the five in SPACE rather than in TIME.** Four shots:
- *2:30–2:31* — locked 21mm wide, giant holds with one foot off the ground. Absolute stillness
  including the dust. One full second of hold. This single beat sells everything after it.
- *2:31–2:32* — 85mm, locked, on the intake seam alone: a line of clean pale light runs along it. One
  element, no move.
- *2:32–2:35.5* — 24mm wide, locked, giant full-figure. **One instruction only: "the figure opens
  outward like a hand unclenching; everything rises slightly before it separates."** Do not name five
  streams here. Just: *it opens.*
- *2:35.5–2:38* — human-height, slow left-to-right pan (a single move) across five **settling** piles:
  cobalt plastic, zinc metal, bottle-green glass, bone-white paper drifting, terracotta wet waste low
  and last.

The money in the money shot is in the **settle**, not the separation. Shooting the settle across a
pan is generable; shooting the separation as five simultaneous flows is not.

Two governing rules for the whole passage: **everything rises before it falls** (rising-then-settling
is the grammar of release; collapsing is the grammar of destruction), and **nothing lands on anyone**
— show a person standing untouched inside the falling paper. Add: no fire, no explosion, no smoke, no
shockwave, nothing thrown toward camera.

### 2. [STRONG · MAJOR] Node 6, 2:32 — the glass veil reads as falling shards

**PROBLEM:** Glass fragments coming down over a riverbank where people are standing is a safety read,
not a beauty read, and Seedance will render "glass" as shards by default.

**FIX:** The glass is never fragments. Whole jars and bottles, and it comes last, descending as a
**slow shimmering curtain that reads as light rather than as objects**, backlit by the low sun,
landing upright and nested. And give the **paper snowfall the most screen time** — backlit drifting
paper over upturned faces against a gold sky is the picture-book image this entire film has been
walking toward. It is also the single most generable element in the sequence. Spend on it.

### 3. [STRONG · BLOCKER] Node 2, 0:47–0:52 — the boy in the netting is the film's highest horror risk

**PROBLEM:** *"tangled to the waist... hands scrabbling at the mesh... going up fast"* — tangled +
scrabbling + a child + a monster is horror grammar. The draft then writes *"like a caught kite,"*
which is the correct image, but it arrives after the damage.

**FIX:** Lead with the kite and rewrite the mechanics around it:
- The net is **loose, large-mesh cargo net**, and he is **held as in a hammock**, not wrapped, wound,
  or constricted.
- **He grabs it himself as it lifts, and hangs on.** A boy holding on reads adventurous. A boy being
  held reads consumed. This one change does more than any lighting note.
- **His face is never obscured by mesh.** He is never inverted, never pressed against a surface.
- **The giant is visibly unaware of him.** He is caught in its clothing like a leaf, not carried as a
  prize. Essential.
- Shoot **85mm from below and beside**, so he reads as a small bright figure against open sky — an
  image of *altitude*, not of *capture*. Absolutely do not shoot this in close-up with mesh across his
  face; that is the one frame that would sink the film.
- **Light him warm and make him the brightest thing in frame**, catching the sunrise, against the
  giant's cool shadow side.
- Two shots maximum in five seconds, and put the emotional weight on the reverse — the street below,
  looking up.

### 4. [STRONG · MAJOR] Node 2 — the giant's face is one instruction away from being a predator

**PROBLEM:** Lorry-headlamp eyes and a bus-grille jaw are excellent design. They are also the two
elements that decide whether this reads as sad or as a monster movie.

**FIX:** Two absolute rules, restated in every node prompt:
- **The headlamp eyes are unlit. They reflect the sky.** Cracked, dull glass catching the sunrise.
  Never an internal glow, never red, never amber-on-black, never tracking. At 2:20, when *"two lorry
  headlamps swing and settle on him,"* what we see is the low sun arriving in the glass — a
  reflection, not an intent.
- **The bus-grille jaw never opens.** Not once, in any node. The giant does not feed with its face; it
  inhales through the throat seam. The draft already separates these — make it explicit and absolute.
  **A jaw that stays shut is a face. A jaw that opens is a mouth.** That single rule removes most of
  the dread risk in the design.

### 5. [STRONG · MAJOR] Node 2, 0:36 onward — the breathing seam must be a vent, not a throat

**PROBLEM:** An opening at the base of a throat that draws things in is, by default, a gullet.
Seedance will happily render wetness, interior darkness, and strands.

**FIX:** Specify it as machinery, and use the word **"intake vent"** — never "mouth," "maw," or
"throat" — in every prompt:
- **Horizontal and shallow**, like a bus radiator grille or a letterbox slot. Never vertical, never
  round.
- **Dry, clean-edged sheet-metal lips.** No wetness, no red, no tissue, no strands, no teeth.
- **Backlit pale** — light passes *through* it, so it reads as an opening to sky rather than an
  interior. The single most important lighting instruction on the creature.
- Its motion is **breath**: slow, steady, tired. Not hunger.

### 6. [STRONG · MAJOR] Node 2, 0:40–0:47 — the swallowing needs visible safety, not stated safety

**PROBLEM:** The draft says *"People run. Nobody is caught by it."* That is on the page; it is not in
the frame. An audience looking at an emptied street and a thing inhaling buses will do the
arithmetic, and the film acquires dread the seed's reconciliation was written to avoid.

**FIX:** **Show people reaching safety inside the same frames** — a shopkeeper pulling a child behind
a half-shutter, a cyclist getting clear at the edge of the shot, a hand pulling someone through a
doorway. Safety must be *seen*. And make the inhale read as vacuum, not appetite: objects go in
**tidily, upright, unhurried, in a line, and nothing crumples, breaks, or crunches on the way.** The
draft's auto-rickshaw with its indicator still blinking all the way in is doing exactly the charter's
work — it is the best image in node 2. Build the other three around its tone.

### 7. [STRONG · MAJOR] All nodes — scale will not survive generation on the strength of "sixty metres"

**PROBLEM:** The draft states 60 m twice and never gives Seedance anything renderable to scale
against. **Numbers do not render.** Six independently generated nodes will produce six
different-sized giants.

**FIX:** Bake these six things into the action lines, restated verbatim per node:
- **Camera height, in metres, in every giant shot: 1.6 m, level.** Never let the camera drift up
  toward the giant's head — the instant the camera is at 60 m the giant looks person-sized and Rajan
  becomes an unreadable ant.
- **Horizon on the lower third line** in all scale shots.
- **A ladder of two known-size objects at different distances**, minimum, between camera and giant:
  near (a railing, an auto-rickshaw, a lamp post), mid (an MTC bus, a four-storey shopfront), far (the
  giant). One reference is not enough; the eye needs depth steps.
- **One architectural comparison per node, stated as a relationship, not a number:** its knee level
  with the Anna flyover deck; its shoulder at the height of the Central clock tower; its head above
  the four-storey hoardings.
- **Aerial perspective up the body:** "haze increasing with height; the head reads two stops flatter
  and bluer than the feet." This sells distance-equals-height better than any other single
  instruction, because it tells the model the head is *far away*.
- **Tempo:** "one stride every three seconds; no fast limb motion; moves at a third of the speed you
  would expect." Seedance's default is human tempo, and that is the number-one thing that will render
  a six-metre giant instead of a sixty-metre one.
- **Put the shake in the world, not the camera.** Dust dropping off a railing, water ringing, a
  hanging cable swinging — a locked camera plus a reacting world outperforms handheld and honours the
  camera rule.

### 8. [STRONG · MAJOR] Nodes 1 and 6 — the rhyme frame must be locked as measurable geometry

**PROBLEM:** Two independent nodes 150 seconds apart cannot be made to match by describing "the same
frame." Also the slug lines contradict each other outright — node 1 is `PRE-DAWN`, the node 6 coda is
`DAWN`, while the action line says *"the same grey-blue light."*

**FIX:** Specify these, character-for-character identical in both nodes:
- **35mm** (not wide — the rhyme is about the same *place*, not the same scale), **camera 1.2 m above
  the sand** (child height, and quietly right), **level, no tilt, 15 m back from the tide line, facing
  east-north-east.**
- **No camera move in either.** A locked frame is the only move Seedance will reproduce twice.
- **Composition as measurable relations:** horizon exactly on the upper third; the lighthouse
  occupying the right third with its top just below the upper third line; the wet tide line running
  lower-left to centre-right.
- **Light, verbatim:** "first light, sun not yet risen above the sea, no direct sun, flat cool
  skylight from the east, sky graduating slate blue at top to pale rose at the horizon."
- **Tide state:** out, wide flat wet sand, low surf. Same in both.
- **Move the burning drone lamp out of this frame.** Give it its own setup at 0:04 so it does not
  contaminate the rhyme.
- **The production instruction that actually guarantees this:** generate the node-1 opening plate once
  at gate 4, and feed that *same reference image* into both node 1 and node 6. The node-6 variant
  (clean sand, crowd behind) is produced by **editing that plate**, never generated from scratch. Flag
  this to gates 4 and 5 now. The rhyme is guaranteed by the shared reference image; the prompt text
  only has to not fight it.
- Permitted differences only: littered sand vs clean sand; empty beach vs beach filling behind; a boy
  running away vs a boy running toward.

### 9. [STRONG · MAJOR] Node 6 is carrying five locations and cannot afford the Marina montage beat

**PROBLEM:** 2:47–2:51 puts Ranganathan Street, the Anna flyover **and Marina Beach** into four
seconds, and the Marina beat — *"a line of people the whole width of the sand"* — directly pre-empts
and competes with the closing image four seconds later. Node 6 is also carrying the sorting, the boy
set down, Meera's gauntlets, the clean Cooum and the coda. It is the most overloaded node in the film.

**FIX:** **Cut Marina out of the montage.** Ranganathan and the flyover only. Marina appears exactly
twice in this film — 0:00 and 2:55 — and its second appearance should be the closing image, alone,
unpreceded. That is what makes the rhyme land. It also buys back a second for the coda, which
currently has five seconds for the most important image in the film.

### 10. [STRONG · MAJOR] The religious-imagery veto has five specific leak points

**PROBLEM:** The draft states the veto correctly in a production note, but **a production note does
not reach Seedance.** Five places will pull religious imagery in unless actively excluded:

**FIX:**
- **(a) Any wide Chennai skyline** — 0:57, 2:52, and every Marina wide. **Never prompt "Chennai
  skyline."** Prompt the specific named buildings only. Keep skylines low, haze-flattened, or occluded
  by the giant and by hoardings. Prefer horizons that are sea, river, or road rather than city.
- **(b) Marina Beach specifically** — the real promenade carries memorial statuary along its length.
  Solve it with framing, not negatives: **"camera faces the sea; the road and promenade are behind
  camera and never in frame."** This is also the correct frame for the rhyme, so it costs nothing.
- **(c) Chennai Central's clock tower** — Indo-Saracenic domes and pointed arches drift toward reading
  as religious architecture in a generated frame. Keep it far background and partial, and **the word
  "station" must be in the prompt** — "a red-brick Victorian railway station with a central clock
  tower, platform sheds and a taxi rank in front." Low prominence.
- **(d) Ranganathan Street hoardings** — real shopfronts carry religious imagery, garlands and
  festival banners. Specify: **"large fabric hoardings in flat saturated colours, abstract patterns
  only, no faces, no text, no imagery."** This also solves Seedance's text-gibberish problem for free.
- **(e) The giant itself — the highest risk and the least obvious.** It is built from a city's
  discarded objects; Seedance will embed a small framed picture, a garland, a bell or a figurine into
  that texture without being asked. Mitigate with a **closed component list**, restated in every node:
  *"made only of corrugated sheet metal, tarpaulin, fishing net, cable, tyres, plastic drums, crates,
  bicycle frames, cooking pots, sandals, hoarding vinyl, PET bottles, cardboard."* A closed list also
  gives creature continuity across six nodes, so it pays twice.

Carry this negative clause **verbatim in all six node prompts**: *"no temples, no gopurams, no domes,
no minarets, no spires, no shrines, no religious statues or idols, no garlands, no festival
decoration, no painted religious symbols, no memorial statuary."*

### 11. [STRONG · MAJOR] The landmarks need their distinguishing features named

**PROBLEM:** "Marina Beach," "Napier Bridge," "the Chennai lighthouse" are names. **Seedance renders
descriptions.** Rajan was explicit that a Chennai audience must recognise these instantly.

**FIX:** the load-bearing detail for each:
- **Marina** — not "a beach." The signature is the *extraordinary width of flat sand* between road and
  water, 300+ metres, which makes the surf look far away and people tiny. State the width.
- **Chennai lighthouse** — **not** a classic white cylindrical lighthouse. A modern **triangular,
  tapering concrete tower, red-and-white banded, with a viewing gallery near the top**, standing at
  the beach edge. Without this you will get Cape Cod.
- **Napier Bridge** — **low and long, not tall**: a multi-arched road bridge crossing the river exactly
  where it meets the sea, with a slender balustrade. *(See fact-checker #12 — the current livery is a
  black-and-white chequerboard.)*
- **The Cooum** — narrow, sluggish, hemmed by low concrete embankments with buildings right at the
  water's edge, and **mats of green water hyacinth**. The hyacinth is the signature.
- **Anna flyover** — a **long sweeping curve** of elevated concrete road over a wide arterial junction,
  hoardings alongside.
- **Ranganathan Street** — the easiest to get wrong and the most recognisable when right: **extremely
  narrow, four-to-six storeys pressed close on both sides, buildings covered top to bottom in giant
  vertical fabric hoardings, dense overhead cable tangle, and no sky visible except a thin slot
  overhead.** That slot is also the entire lighting design of node 3.
- **Kuppai medu** — **flat-topped** hills of compacted refuse, taller than the four-storey buildings
  around them, kite birds circling, heat haze.

### 12. [STRONG · MAJOR] Node 3, 1:14–1:22 — the barrage is ungenerable and the closest the film comes to a war movie

**PROBLEM:** Twenty exo-frames, rail slugs, drone fire and foam charges in one narrow-street shot is a
many-agent, high-motion compound. Seedance will produce chaos, and the charter would rather not have a
combat sequence at all.

**FIX:** **Never show the weapons firing in wide.** Render it as light and reaction, in three simple
shots: the street goes white (locked wide, no visible projectiles, no muzzles — just the wash); 85mm
on Arul's face lit by strobing white light from off-screen; low-angle on the giant's plating taking
impacts as bright soft blooms, **no visible projectiles.** Three shots, same beat, generable, and
about ninety percent less "war film."

Also: **remove the word "burning"** from *"fall burning-slow, like paper."* **Nothing burns anywhere
in this film.** Fire brings its own palette and its own dread. *"Falling slow, like paper"* is enough
and is better. And reduce twenty exo-frames to **three legible in the foreground, the line continuing
as silhouettes into haze.** Same at 2:46 — three pilots, the row receding.

### 13. [STRONG · MAJOR] Node 5 — two action clusters are too dense to generate

**PROBLEM:** *2:12–2:20:* five objects found, washed, dried and laid out in eight seconds. Five
distinct actions; Seedance will render one. *2:20–2:27:* four throws, each with a different
deflection, in seven seconds. Seedance will render one throw.

**FIX**, and it costs no information:
- **The washing:** show ONE object completely — the glass jar, 85mm, water running off it until it
  runs clear, dried on a sleeve, set down on the mud. Then one shot of the other four **already laid
  out in a row.** One action rendered beautifully plus a result shot. The draft's line — *"it is the
  most careful thing in the film"* — is only true if we actually see one thing done carefully.
- **The throws:** one throw shown fully (the bottle, spinning away off the new plating); then one
  wider shot with three objects falling away at once; then the row of four wet marks in the mud where
  the objects were. Three shots, same beat, and the empty row is a better image than the fourth throw.

### 14. [SUGGESTION · MAJOR] There is no close-up economy, and the film's most important image has no framing

**PROBLEM:** Nodes 1–3 are wide-heavy and we never see Arul's face large before 1:52. Meanwhile
**2:27–2:30, the open hand — the single most important image in the film — gets three seconds and no
camera specified at all.** And 2:20's *"a man the size of a thumbnail standing under a weather
system"* spends the emotional peak on a shot in which his face is unreadable.

**FIX:** Declare a close-up budget of **four**, so that when one arrives it is enormous:
1. his hands turning the wrapper, 0:10
2. his face in the white weapon-light, 1:18
3. his shaking hands, 1:54
4. **the open palm, 2:29**

(plus Kavi's face at 0:50, his only one.) Then give #4 the frame it deserves: **85mm, from slightly
below, his fist against open sky, the giant's underside filling the top of frame out of focus, no move
or a very slow rise, and the folded yellow square the only saturated colour in the frame** — the
payoff of the palette discipline set at 0:03. Give it four seconds, not three (Finding 9 buys them).
And at 2:20 take both shots: the true scale wide *and* an 85mm on his face. Do not spend the peak on
the thumbnail alone.

### 15. [SUGGESTION · MAJOR] The Cooum should not be black

**PROBLEM:** "Black water, flat as tar" plus chest-deep wading plus a 60 m figure displacing it is a
hard fluid render, and "black" pulls Seedance toward oil-slick and tar — dread-adjacent, and
incidentally not what the Cooum looks like.

**FIX:** **Deep opaque olive-green, dull sheen, mats of water hyacinth.** Truer, far more renderable,
less grim — and it sets up 2:51 as the same hue moving from dull to clear rather than a colour swap,
which is a much stronger transformation.

### 16. [SUGGESTION · MAJOR] Node 1, 0:12–0:22 — the little knot is the second horror risk

**PROBLEM:** *"A clot of wet litter... welded together out of rot and rag... hunching itself along the
sand."* Small, wet, crawling things are creepy. And "rot," "fermenting," "wet-packed" appearing
anywhere near an image prompt will pull Seedance toward slime.

**FIX:** The knot **hops and trundles**; it is **dry and papery**, not wet; it has **no discernible
face**; and Arul's total indifference — he is thinking about breakfast — is what tells the audience how
to feel. Shoot it **50mm at his eye height looking down, in the same frame as his boots** — an ordinary
object in an ordinary morning. **Never in macro close-up.** Prompt language: *"a tumbling knot of
paper, film and rag the size of a football, hopping along the sand."*

General note for the whole film: keep **"rot," "fermenting," "wet-packed"** in the beat sheet where
they belong and **out of every image prompt.** In prompts use **compacted, baled, pressed.**

### 17. [SUGGESTION · MODERATE] The giant's silhouette needs stated rules

**PROBLEM:** The design is right but its *posture* is unspecified, and posture is what decides sad
versus predatory.

**FIX**, restated per node: **shoulders rounded and forward, head hung slightly, long hanging arms,
blunt round hands** (tyres for knuckles is already correct — keep the hands blunt). **No claws, no
spines, no horns, no spikes.** The reference shape is **a loaded porter or a labouring elephant**, not
a beast. Torn hoardings hanging off it like banners is the draft's best pathos detail — increase it.
And **it should visibly sag and shed**: a thing constantly losing small pieces of itself reads as sad
and tired, which is exactly what 2:30 needs the audience to already feel.

On colour and texture: **desaturated greys, rust-browns, faded tarpaulin blues, bleached hoarding
pastels — and individual objects readable at every scale.** You should be able to find a bicycle, a
cooking pot, a sandal in it. **Readable component objects read as pathos; an undifferentiated brown
mass reads as grime and dread.** State: *"texture, not grime; no undifferentiated sludge, no dripping
black slime."*

### 18. [SUGGESTION · MODERATE] Two continuity items that will drift across six nodes

- **Lettering.** *"Each lid hand-lettered"* and *"a canvas pouch lettered with a single question
  mark."* Seedance renders text as garbage. **Kill the hand-lettered lids** — the bins are identified
  by colour only, which is the whole point of Deliverable 2 anyway. **Keep the question mark**: a
