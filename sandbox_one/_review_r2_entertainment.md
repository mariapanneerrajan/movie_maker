## FINDINGS

### 1. [STRONG] Node 5, 2:22–2:28 — four failure modes in six seconds is worse than four identical failures in seven

**PROBLEM:** The failure modes are correct and the "he counts the seam" beat is excellent. But this
is **1.5 seconds per throw**, *less* than v1's 1.75, while carrying four times the information. Each
throw must now show the throw, the flight, a *specific* reason for failure, and him walking closer.
Four events in 1.5 seconds. It will render as a blur — and a blur that contains distinctions the
audience cannot resolve is worse than a blur that contains none, because the viewer registers "he
keeps failing" *and* registers that they missed something.

Also a logic break introduced in the revision. ONE is now "thrown long. It goes out over the seam
entirely and away." Then: "He walks closer." **You do not walk closer after an overthrow.** The whole
point of throw one was that distance defeats him, which is what motivates the walk. Overthrowing
motivates walking back.

**FIX:** Restore throw one to a **short**: "The bottle goes up, dies before it gets there, and falls
back into the mud. He is too far away." Now the walk is the obvious correction and the runner has a
motor for all four. Expand 2:22–2:28 to **2:20–2:29, nine seconds, ~2.2s each.** Seconds come from
two static beats in the same node — cut "PAST THE SUITS" 5s→3s (finding 5) and "THE EMPTY POUCH"
3s→2s. Both are men standing still. The throws are the film's last action sequence and the thing that
earns the open hand.

### 2. [STRONG] Node 5, 2:26–2:28 — throw four must be a visible REFUSAL, not a bounce

**PROBLEM:** The biggest craft opportunity in the draft, currently one clause. The newspaper block
has been washed and dried. **By the film's own stated RULE it should work.** It goes in "dead centre"
— and the seam "shuts on it, and coughs it back out." An attentive viewer asks, correctly, *why did
the clean newspaper fail and the clean wrapper work?* If the answer is "because it was thrown, not
given," that is the entire theology of the film rendered as a physical gesture — and the draft never
makes it legible. As written, a storyboard artist will draw a bounce, and **a bounce reads as bad
luck.**

**FIX:** Make it a **choice by the giant, visible in one frame.** The seam is wide open. The block is
dead centre and going in. And the seam **shuts against it** — deliberate, unhurried, the way a mouth
closes against a spoon. It is not deflected. It is *declined*, and the block drops at his feet.

Then at 2:28 the contrast is already written and lands like a hammer: "The plating parts wide — the
seam, drawing in air and dust and the whole morning." **The giant refuses what is fired at it and
accepts what is offered to it.** Force fails, offering works, two gestures, no dialogue — and it
retroactively explains node 3, because the barrage was the same refusal at scale. Do not let this
stay as a bounce.

### 4. [STRONG] Node 6, 2:47–2:51 — the second-giant synthesis is right and is budgeted to fail

**THE JUDGEMENT FIRST, since you asked me to be blunt: the synthesis is not having it both ways, and
it is better than my round-1 version. I withdraw my objection.** "They walked out to fight and
arrived to carry" is a genuinely finer beat than "they won the rematch," it does not repeat node 5,
and "some of them stop walking to watch" is a real image. A crowd not doing something is *not* a
satisfying climax — but **this is not the climax.** The climax is the open hand at 2:29 and the
sorting at 2:33. This is denouement, and denouement is exactly where "the work turns out to be
already done" belongs. **The head writer won this one.**

What is broken is the **budget** and one **logic seam**.

*Budget:* four seconds is asked to carry — second giant still standing, city walking north with empty
sacks, light arrives, giant resolves on the horizon, crowd still walking, some stop to watch, they
arrive, they carry, flatbeds. Eight to nine images at roughly 0.5 seconds each. **The reversal cannot
land, because a reversal needs a setup the audience has time to worry about.** You need at minimum
**1.5 uninterrupted seconds of "there is another one and he is spent and there are thousands of
people walking toward it"** before the light arrives. Right now the worry and the relief are in the
same half-second and they cancel.

*Logic seam:* at 2:36 the light "runs on — north, faster than anyone could follow it." At 2:47 a
crowd has walked most of ten kilometres on foot and the light has not arrived yet. The film has told
us the light is faster than people and then shown it slower than people.

**FIX — one change solves both.** Do not make the light fast. Make it **exactly the speed of a
walking crowd.** At 2:36 it runs out of the giant, over the riverbed, and north — a thin bright seam
moving across the ground at a steady, patient, human pace, and out of frame. Then at 2:47 the shot is
not "crowd" and then "light." It is **one shot: thousands of people walking north up the flyover with
empty sacks, and running along the ground beside them, keeping pace, the line of clean light.** They
are walking next to the thing that is doing the work. It gets there first by a few hundred metres,
the giant resolves on the horizon, and some of them stop to watch.

Same theology said better — **labour walks alongside grace and never ahead of it** — the speed
contradiction is gone, you get a single sustained image instead of six chopped ones, and the crowd is
*active in frame* while still not being the cause. Budget **7 seconds, 2:46–2:53.** Give it one face:
one cutaway of a woman with an empty sack stopping, and lowering it, is worth more than the flatbeds.

### 6. [SUGGESTION] Node 4, 1:50–1:54 — "THE BEACH GETS UP" works, but Arul is not in it

**PROBLEM:** The beat lands in the right place — after he refuses the armour, before his hands shake,
so it *causes* the shaking. Correct construction, and the best new image in the draft. But it is
written as a cutaway: "South, along Marina: every small knot on that beach turns at once." **Nobody is

**FIX:** Put him in the frame and the beach *at his feet.* We are at the Cooum mouth, which is the
north end of Marina — same sand. Shoot over his shoulder: he is standing in the mud in his wet greens,
having just climbed out of the exo-frame, and **the knots come past him.** Thousands, trundling and

he swept this morning walk into the giant. Then cut to his hands.

That converts the film's second-biggest image from spectacle into low point, makes node 1's "one
wrapper bigger" pay off as arithmetic the audience does themselves, and justifies "he sits down
because his legs have decided it" — right now that reaction is a size ahead of what he was shown. Give

### 8. [SUGGESTION] Node 5, 2:06 / 2:19 — the returning wrapper reopens the self-sourcing question by the back door

water he was afraid of, six seconds after he asked. That is grace, it is a better beat than either
alternative, and it costs nothing.

## WHAT'S WORKING

- **"Every round you fire, it --" cut off by "Behind the line."** The truncation landed exactly as
  hoped. He never finishes, the audience finishes it fourteen seconds later, and being interrupted
  *is* his character in this node. **Do not let anyone restore the second half of that line at any
  downstream gate.**
- **The spent casing at 1:16.** "It is tacky. A scrap of foil from the gutter has already bonded
  itself to the side of it and will not come off when he pulls." He crouches "because of course he
  does." **The best new writing in v2** — it makes him active without giving him a swing, and converts
  the last four seconds of the barrage from noise into suspense.
- **The slugs tiling on in flight, 1:12–1:16.** "They SINK IN, and settle, and tile themselves flat
  across its chest in overlapping rows, spreading outward like frost taking a window." Live, legible,
  family-safe, and unlike any monster film I can name. **Protect the word *frost*.**
- **"He watches the seam open, and shut, and open, and shut, and he counts it."** This one sentence is
  what makes the four misses read as a craftsman being defeated by geometry rather than a small man
  being silly. Load-bearing for finding 1. **Do not cut it when the throws are re-timed.**
- **The useful gauntlets at 2:44.** "One frame goes down on both knees to hold a sack steady for a
  nine-year-old, who fills it without looking up." That last clause — *without looking up* — is what
  makes it funny instead of sentimental. The child does not find it remarkable. **Keep the clause.**
- **"Nobody is sorting anything. It is already sorted. They are gathering it."** Direction, not image,
  but it is the correct instruction to every downstream stage and it is the difference between the
  ending being gratitude and the ending being payment.
- **The pouch arc** — filled by Arul (0:14), emptied by the river (2:06), refilled by the boy (2:58).
  Genuinely better than v1's dead pouch beat, and now the film's quietest structure.
- **Everything protected in the head writer's list.** The auto-rickshaw indicator, RESOLVES as the
  verb, "it falls without a crash, it sits down," the sad giant, both gauntlets, "a row of chairs
  after everyone has gone home," "he has one thing left in his hand and it is a sweet wrapper," Kavi's
