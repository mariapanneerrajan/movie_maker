## FINDINGS

### 1. [BLOCKING] Node 2, 0:30 and Node 6, 2:54 — the flagstaff is not teak and is not the tallest

**CLAIM:** "the Fort St George flagstaff -- a hundred and fifty feet of teak, the tallest in the
country -- with the flag up in the first of the light."

**FINDING:** Two errors in one clause.
**(a) Not teak.** The original teak mast — cut from a ship's mast wrecked off Madras, erected by
Elihu Yale in 1688 — stood until **1994, when it was replaced by a steel/metal replica.** What flies
the flag today is metal. Sources conflict and I flag rather than pick silently: Wikipedia still says
"Made of teakwood, it is 46 metres (150 ft) high," and tourist sites copy that line, but the
sentence describes the original and carries no date; the dated, specific account (mast stood
1688–1994, then a steel replica installed under CM Jayalalithaa, the original beam cut up and stored
in the Fort) comes from the Madras heritage record and is corroborated in the news. **The dated
account wins.**
**(b) Not the tallest.** Every serious source says "**one of** the tallest flagstaffs in the
country." India's tallest flagpole is Attari–Wagah, **418 ft (2023)**; before that Belagavi, **361
ft / 110 m (2018)**. A 150 ft mast is a third of that. In a film set fifteen years from now the
superlative is comprehensively false.
**(c)** Height itself is fine: 150 ft (46 m) is the common figure; the heritage record gives **148
ft**. Either is defensible.

**SOURCES:** sriramv.com/2015/05/11/know-fort-st-george-2-the-flagstaff · thesouthfirst.com ·
en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_St._George (the conflicting source) · currentaffairs.adda247.com,
tribuneindia.com (Belagavi 361 ft 2018; Attari 418 ft 2023)

**FIX:** Delete both false words, keep the image: **"the Fort St George flagstaff -- a hundred and
fifty feet of it, one of the tallest in the country -- with the flag up in the first of the light."**
If the room wants the teak (it is a lovely fact), attribute it to time, not the present object:
*"where a teak mast stood for three hundred years."*

### 2. [BLOCKING] Node 3, 1:05 — twenty exo-frames cannot stand abreast on Usman Road either

**CLAIM:** "Twenty exo-frames fill Usman Road kerb to kerb -- three metres each..."

**FINDING:** The round-1 arithmetic survives the move to a bigger road. Twenty machines three metres
across need **~60 m of clear frontage**; at IRC's urban lane width of **3.5 m** that is roughly **17
traffic lanes.** Even on the draft's own claim of six lanes — 21 m of carriageway — the line holds
**six abreast at absolute maximum**, with no gaps and no footpath. If "three metres" is height rather
than width and the shoulder is ~1.8 m, twenty abreast still needs 36–40 m, i.e. ten to eleven lanes.
Nothing in T. Nagar is close: OSM classifies both North and South Usman Road as
`highway=secondary`/`tertiary`, entirely one-way, with service lanes press reporting describes as
"fully inaccessible" through encroachment. The script is also internally ambiguous about whether
three metres is height or frontage, which will produce twenty different answers at gate 4.

**SOURCES:** civilnotess.com / IRC carriageway specs (3.5 m per urban lane; 7.0–7.5 m two-lane) ·
OpenStreetMap/Overpass, ways "North Usman Road" (13 ways, all one-way) and "South Usman Road" (16
ways, all one-way, several `lanes=1`) · dtnext.in T. Nagar encroachment reporting

**FIX:** Name the formation and the problem disappears, with a better shot: **"Twenty exo-frames fill
Usman Road kerb to kerb -- four abreast and five ranks deep, three metres each, rail-cannon slung,
hydraulics venting."** The count survives, "kerb to kerb" becomes true (4 × 3 m = 12 m, a real
carriageway), "he ends up behind twenty rail-cannon" still reads, and **a column in depth is a more
frightening thing to walk a small man past than a chorus line.**

### 3. [STRONG] Node 3, 1:00 — "the widest road in the district, six lanes and a tree line"

**FINDING:** I could not find a single published carriageway width, right-of-way or lane count for
Usman Road in GCC, CMDA, Smart City, Mega Streets or ITDP material, and I looked in all five. What
*is* documented is that Usman Road is **one of six arterial streets of equal rank** converging on
Panagal Park (North Usman, South Usman, G. N. Chetty, Sir Thyagaraya, Venkatanarayana, Doraiswamy) —
so the singular superlative has five rivals and no source. On the tree line: T. Nagar is losing
canopy, not carrying it — **Panagal Park alone lost over 30% of its green cover between 2011 and
2025**, and Chennai Metro has felled 5,472 trees against a projected 2,043. Usman Road is a
shopfront-and-hoarding canyon. This is exactly the standard on which I refused the Chennai Central
sightline in round 1, and I am applying it to myself.

**SOURCES:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._Nagar (Panagal Park, the six arterial streets) ·
citizenmatters.in, "How infrastructure projects are costing Chennai its green cover"

**FIX:** Swap the superlative for the sourced fact, which is more local and costs nothing: **"One of
the six arterial roads that meet at Panagal Park -- and running off it the mouth of Ranganathan
Street, the most crowded street in the city, and it is empty."** Drop "six lanes and a tree line." If
the room specifically wants trees on camera, the one T. Nagar street with documented planted trees is
**Sir Thyagaraya Road (the Pondy Bazaar pedestrian plaza)** — but its carriageway there is only 7 m,
so the column is four abreast in any case.

### 4. [STRONG] Node 6, 2:35 vs 2:47 — the light is stated fast and dramatised slow

**CLAIM:** 2:35 — "It runs on -- out along the riverbed, over the ground, north, **faster than anyone
could follow it.**" 2:47 — "Ten kilometres north, the Kodungaiyur giant is **still standing**... And
before the nearest of them is within a kilometre of it, the clean light comes over the ground and
reaches it."

**FINDING:** Internal, and load-bearing, because this beat carries the theological point. Between the
two lines the film plays: a boy set down, Arul in the shallows, Meera pulling off both gauntlets and
washing a bottle, twenty pilots climbing back into frames, children and shopkeepers filling sacks by
the armload, and an entire city assembling and walking out onto the Anna flyover. That is an hour of
story time at the very least. Anything "faster than anyone could follow" covers ten kilometres many
times over inside it — the second giant should be down before Meera says "Bags." As staged, the light
travels at roughly the speed of the crowd. **The film states one speed and dramatises another**, and
the head writer's note repeats the fast version.

**FIX:** One clause, and it improves the theme: **"out along the riverbed, over the ground, north --
no faster than a man walks."** Then the light and the city move north at the same pace and grace
still arrives a kilometre ahead of the first pair of hands. That is precisely what the beat is for,
and it removes the contradiction without touching another line.

### 5. [SUGGESTION] Node 6, 2:51 — the capped outfall needs somewhere for the sewage to go

**FINDING:** The intervention is the right one and the film now tells the truth about the mechanism.
But the real method is **interception and diversion**: the outfall is plugged *and* the flow is taken
into an interceptor sewer and carried to treatment. The Integrated Cooum River Eco-Restoration
Project (Rs 735.08 crore, 32 km from the Paruthipattu check dam to the river mouth) is **10 I&D
systems, 3 modular STPs at Chetpet, College Road and Maduravoyal, a 10 MLD STP at Langs Garden and a
UGSS at Nerkundram.** A cap with nothing shown receiving the flow can read as "they corked it," which
would put sewage into the streets.

**SOURCES:** crrt.tn.gov.in/cooum_restoration · greentribunal.gov.in OA 265/2016 · groundreport.in

**FIX:** "…capped -- a Corporation crew on the last bolts, the pipe dry, **and the flow going away
west down a new interceptor line.**"

### 6. [SUGGESTION] Node 6, 2:52 — aeration is real, but a trash boom would be truer and better

**FINDING:** Not a token and not a fabrication — **aeration with in-situ bioremediation of urban
drains is genuinely practised in India** (NMCG Sustainable River Rejuvenation pilots; published field
studies), and the film's sequencing is correct: you aerate *after* the load stops, not instead of
stopping it. But it does not appear in the documented Cooum works. What GCC has actually put into
these waterways is **trash booms — at Kotturpuram, Maraimalai Adigal Bridge and Jafferkhanpet** —
which is also the better image for this film, because a trash boom is literally the city catching
what it dropped before it reaches the sea, which is the whole thesis of the ending.

**SOURCES:** citizenmatters.in, CRRT restoration strategy · thestatesman.com, NMCG pilots ·
propulsiontechjournal.com, "Aeration and In-Situ Bioremediation of Municipal Drains"

**FIX:** Keep the aeration if the room wants white water on camera — it is defensible. Consider
pairing or replacing it with a trash boom stretched across the channel with a crew working it.

### 7. [SUGGESTION] The flagstaff sightline holds — but "immediately north" is 1.2 km, and St Mary's spire is a veto risk

**(a) The sightline: I certify it.** The flagstaff has stood since the early 1800s on the **"Great
Bulwark" above the Sea Gate** — the fort's seaward, eastern face — and existed to be read by ships in
the Madras roadstead. Napier Bridge sits at 13.0688 N, 80.2845; the Fort St George polygon runs
13.0744–13.0849 N, centre 13.0797. That is **about 1.2 km due north over open coastal ground**, with
the mast raised on a bulwark and, in the heritage record's words, "towering over the rest of the
mostly low topography." A 45 m mast at 1.2 km subtends about two degrees and clears the fort's low
buildings. Materially different from Chennai Central's 41 m tower at 2.2 km sitting behind the fort,
the Island Grounds tree line and the Ripon Building, which I refused in round 1.

**(b) But it is not "immediately."** "Immediately north" will put the flagstaff a hundred metres from
the giant in the storyboard. Say **"A kilometre north,"** and note for gates 3–5 that the mast is
**45 m and the giant is 60 m** — the giant is taller than the tallest flagstaff in Madras, which is a
gift of a composition if the panel is built knowing it.

**(c) VETO WATCH for the standing note. St Mary's Church stands inside Fort St George** — 1680, the
oldest Anglican church in India and "the first east of Suez," with a west tower and a tall spire
making "a picturesque silhouette against the backdrop of the fort." **Any reference image prompted
with "Fort St George" will produce that spire.** The fort's *use* is entirely secular (Tamil Nadu
Secretariat and Legislative Assembly) and the flagstaff is a national flag on a government seat, so
the landmark itself passes — but the note must now say: **Fort St George appears only as the
flagstaff and the ramparts; no church tower or spire in frame.** Same class of risk as the round-1
Santhome/Kapaleeshwarar watch item, and it travels with the image prompts, not the script.

**SOURCES:** sriramv.com · OpenStreetMap/Overpass (Napier Bridge ways 24888589/28295242;
Fort St George way 1178009706) · en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mary's_Church,_Chennai ·
en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_St._George

### 8. [SUGGESTION] Node 6, 2:35 — compost is not terracotta

**FINDING:** The underlying claim is sound — composting is the prescribed treatment for the wet
fraction; finished compost is **dark brown to black, crumbly, holding its shape when squeezed and
breaking easily, with a clean earthy smell**, and a pile is genuinely **warm (50–70 °C) while
active**, cooling to ambient at maturity. So "warm," "dark," "crumbling" and "clean" are all right.
**"Terracotta" is the wrong pigment** — terracotta is orange-red, and an image model given that word
will build an orange hill.

**SOURCES:** planetnatural.com, gardenerbible.com (finished compost colour/crumb/temperature) ·
pib.gov.in SWM Rules 2026 notification; SWM Rules 2016

**FIX:** "…lands as **warm dark-brown earth, crumbling and clean**." Same rhythm, right material.

### 10. [SUGGESTION] Node 5, 2:26 — why throw four fails is available but never said

**FINDING:** Not a rule break — there is a clean reading and it is a good one. The stated rule is
that the mass cannot bind through a washed, dried thing; so a clean object *thrown* into the seam
cannot be gripped and is spat straight back out. The fifth works because it is **inhaled** — drawn in
past the point where it can be expelled. The film obeys this exactly. But it never says it, and the
difference between throw four (dead centre, into the open seam, rejected) and the open hand is the
entire hinge of the ending.

**FIX:** One clause at 2:27, in action not dialogue: *"It lands in the mud at his feet. Nothing
sticks to a clean thing when it is thrown."*
