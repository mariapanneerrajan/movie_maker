# Round 1 — entertainment-doctor (advisory, no veto)

**Draft reviewed:** `_draft_v1.md`
**VERDICT: REVISE**

**THE HOOK:** A sixty-metre giant made of a city's own garbage gets BIGGER every time the army
shoots it — and the only man who can stop it walks in unarmed carrying a washed sweet wrapper. That
hook is real and it is excellent. It is also **not visible until 1:22**, which is 72 seconds after
the audience decided whether to stay.

---

## FINDINGS

### 1. [STRONG] NODE 1 · 0:00–0:06

**PROBLEM:** The film opens on six seconds of landscape. "Grey-blue light over the Bay of Bengal.
The tide line runs with litter... The lighthouse stands off to the south." Nothing moves, nothing is
strange, and nothing is at stake. Then a boy drops a wrapper — a real image, but a small one, and it
does not tell a scrolling stranger that this film has a sixty-metre monster in it. Six seconds is
3.3% of the runtime spent on a postcard. The single most arresting thing in Node 1 — the
football-sized clot of wet litter that HUNCHES ITSELF ALONG THE SAND, "dragging a straw and a lid
into its own body as it goes" — is sitting at 0:12. **That is your opening image and it is twelve
seconds late.**

**FIX:** Restructure the first ten seconds and do not spend a frame on establishing.
- *0:00–0:02* — MACRO, hard on the wrapper in flight. Mango-yellow foil tumbling in slow motion
  against flat grey water. Gorgeous, abstract, instantly a thumbnail.
- *0:02–0:05* — It lands on wet sand. Something crawls into frame for it: the knot. It absorbs the
  wrapper — the wrapper is visibly EATEN, and the knot gets slightly bigger. In five seconds the
  audience has (a) beauty, (b) a creature, (c) the entire rule of the film: litter feeds the thing.
  No dialogue, no exposition.
- *0:05–0:10* — The picker comes in and takes the wrapper back OUT of the knot. Now Arul's first
  action is a small rescue, not a sweep. Introduce the cart, the five lids, the joggers passing
  "through him like he is weather."

The opening/closing rhyme is untouched — same beach, same light, same wrapper, same framing. You
have only put the strange thing first, where it belongs.

### 2. [STRONG] NODE 4 · 1:30–2:00 — the spectacle vacuum

**PROBLEM:** This is the only node in the film with no large image in it. Exo-frames come back, a
hatch opens, a man cannot close a glove, a man sits on a kerb, a boy shouts. It is emotionally
correct and visually the flattest thirty seconds in the script, arriving directly after the loudest
thirty (Node 3's barrage). A film that plateaus at Node 3 loses people at Node 4 — this one doesn't
plateau, it drops. Worse, the giant's threat goes ENTIRELY off screen: "The giant wades the Cooum in
the middle distance, unhurried, turning for the sea. For the beach. For where the litter is
thickest." That is the most frightening sentence in the draft and it exists only as scene direction.
Nobody watching sees it.

**FIX:** Put one enormous image at 1:50–1:54 and make it the payoff of Node 1's knot. Cut to Marina
Beach: every small knot on the sand — dozens, then hundreds — has turned and is crawling seaward in
one direction, streaming down the wet sand into the Cooum mouth and UP the giant's legs, being
drunk in. The giant grows visibly as we watch. It is not attacking; it is FEEDING, on the beach we
opened the film on.

This does four jobs at once: it escalates Node 4 above Node 3 (the enemy is now unkillable AND
growing on a clock), it pays off the knot from 0:03, it makes the black water Arul stares at in the
last two seconds the same water we saw across the surf at 0:00, and it is a genuinely new image no
other giant-monster film has. Geography holds — the Cooum empties beside Napier Bridge at the north
end of Marina, so beach and river mouth are the same place, and Node 5's location is unbroken.

*Budget:* take the 4 seconds from finding 6, and tighten 1:37–1:45 ("Then you wear it") from 8s to
6s — it is a hatch and one line.

### 3. [STRONG] NODE 5 · 2:20–2:27 — the four throws

**PROBLEM:** Two problems, one of them a logic hole. **(a)** 1.75 seconds per throw will blur; four
identical failures read as one long failure, and the runner never builds. **(b)** The bigger issue:
the film established at 0:12–0:22 and re-established at 1:22 that clean sorted things go to the
SEAM, and that the plating is now impenetrable. So watching Arul throw four objects into the plating
makes him look like he has forgotten his own mechanism. Four proofs that he doesn't understand his
own idea, then a fifth where he suddenly does. Also, tonally, a small man lobbing a bottle at a
monster four times risks reading as comic pathos twelve seconds before the film's holiest beat.

**FIX:** He is aiming at the seam every single time. The seam is what defeats him, not his own
error. Expand to 9 seconds, ~2.2s each, and give each throw a different, escalating reason for
failure so the runner builds:
- **ONE (bottle)** — thrown from distance. Falls short. He is too far away. He walks closer.
- **TWO (tumbler)** — good line, but the giant EXHALES and the seam closes flush as it arrives.
  Deflected off a tyre knuckle. He walks closer.
- **THREE (jar)** — the giant turns; the seam swings away from him. Gone into the netting. He walks
  closer — now he is directly beneath it.
- **FOUR (newspaper block)** — this one is dead centre and it is going IN, and the seam shuts on it
  and spits it back. It lands in the water at his feet.

Now he is out of things and standing directly under the intake, and the ONLY reason the fifth works
is that he stops trying to hit it and lets it be taken. **Force fails; offering works.** That is
your theme, delivered as physics, with no line of dialogue. Four escalating near-misses is a classic
runner and it reads. Four identical misses is a montage.

### 4. [STRONG] NODE 3 · 1:14–1:30 — the best idea in the film is hidden behind smoke

**PROBLEM:** The giant wearing its own incoming fire as new armour is the most inventive image the
script has, and the draft delivers it as a static after-the-fact reveal: "The smoke clears. The
giant is BIGGER." We are told what happened; we never see it happen. Separately, Arul has nothing to
do for these sixteen seconds, which is where the deliberate Node 3 deviation actually costs you —
not at 1:08 where he's fighting to be heard (that plays), but here, where he is furniture.

**FIX:** Two changes, both cheap.
- **(a)** Show the absorption in flight. Mid-barrage, one shot at 1:18, high speed: rail slugs
  arrive at the chest and do not spark or ricochet — they SINK IN, and tile themselves, click,
  click, click, into a shining new layer of scale-mail, spreading outward from every impact like
  frost. The audience understands the rule live instead of being told it afterward, and it is a
  spectacular, family-safe, picture-book image.
- **(b)** Give Arul the dramatic irony. At 1:16, a spent slug casing bounces to a stop at his feet.
  He crouches — of course he does, he picks things up — turns it over, and it is already tacky with
  rot, already bonding. He knows before anyone. Cut to his face. Now the last six seconds of the
  barrage are suspense instead of noise, because one man in the frame already knows what is about to
  be revealed and nobody will hold still for him.

His stated reason for staying passive holds — he never gets a swing — but he must never be inert.
**Reaction is action at this runtime.**

### 5. [STRONG] 0:57 SECOND GIANT — a hanging gun, and the fix that saves the ending

**PROBLEM:** The second giant rises at 0:57, is on screen for roughly three seconds, and does not
appear again for one hundred and fourteen seconds, at which point it is already being dismantled. An
audience will not carry a three-second image that far. As written it is a loose thread. And its
absence is exactly why the 2:47–2:55 cleanup montage is at risk of playing as a public-service
announcement: **nothing in the film's plot REQUIRES the city to clean up.** Arul already won. The
montage becomes an epilogue about being tidy.

**FIX:** Bring the second giant back once, at 1:56, in the same frame as Arul's shaking hands — a
second silhouette on the north horizon, walking toward the first. Now the film has posed a question
the audience can do the arithmetic on: one man, two giants, and he can barely hold a picker. When
Arul brings the first one down at 2:30, the second is still standing — and the city-wide cleanup at
2:47 is no longer a moral; it is the ONLY remaining way to win, and the audience already knows it
before the film shows it. The kuppa medu hills "being taken down and rebuilt as five neat coloured
mountains" at 2:51 stops being a nice image and becomes the second victory. Same shots, same
runtime, completely different function. **This is the highest-leverage change in the review.**

### 6. [SUGGESTION] NODE 1 · 0:22–0:26 — "THE POUCH" is a dead beat

**PROBLEM:** Four seconds of a man patting a pouch flat, conveying information the film already gave
at 0:10 ("a thing that belongs to none of the five. He tucks it into the pouch"). It is the only
second of this film I would not miss, and there are four of them.

**FIX:** Cut the beat. Keep the pouch by adding a one-second insert inside the 0:05–0:10 block:
close on the hand-lettered question mark as the wrapper goes in. Bank the other three seconds toward
finding 2. Also trim 1:00–1:08 to five seconds — the empty Ranganathan Street is a strong image but
it does not need eight seconds to land — and bank two more.

### 7. [SUGGESTION] NODE 6 · 2:47–2:51 — the montage needs one laugh and one payoff

**PROBLEM:** "MARINA BEACH: a line of people the whole width of the sand, moving together toward the
water, bending, straightening, bending." That is a stock inspirational image and it is the one shot
in the montage that will read as a lecture. The other two are specific and therefore good —
especially "Someone hands a bottle across a bonnet to a stranger."

**FIX:** Replace the beach line with the payoff the film has already set up and not cashed. The
twenty exo-frames are still standing there. Put the pilots back in them — and have them use those
useless three-inch gauntlets to HOLD SACKS OPEN while barefoot kids and shopkeepers fill them. The
most expensive war machines in Chennai, reduced to the humblest possible job, and finally good at
something. It is funny, it is warm, it directly answers "I can't work in this" from 1:45, and it
says "the last shall be first" without a single word. One shot. Put it first in the montage so the
audience laughs, and then the flyover and the river land on an open audience instead of a braced
one.

### 8. [SUGGESTION] DIALOGUE — two of eight lines

**PROBLEM:** Six of the eight are load-bearing and clean. Two are not.
- **MEERA: "Work."** at 1:48. The direction "She says it like a foreign word" is unplayable and the
  line is ambiguous on screen — it can read as contempt, confusion, or an order, and the audience
  has no time to resolve it.
- **ARUL: "Every round you fire -- it keeps."** at 1:11 is the only line in the film that explains a
  thing rather than does a thing.

**FIX:**
- Cut "Work." entirely. Let Meera say nothing and simply watch him walk past her. This also gives
  her exactly three lines, all of them commands, in a perfect arc: "Behind the line." → "Then you
  wear it." → "Bags." The third is the humble one. Her whole character in three imperatives.
- Truncate Arul's line so he never finishes it: "Every round you fire, it —" and land MEERA's
  "Behind the line." on top of him. He is cut off mid-sentence, which is the entire point of his
  character in this node, and the audience completes the sentence themselves fourteen seconds later
  when the smoke clears. **A rule the audience deduces is worth ten times a rule they were told.**
- Keep "I can't work in this." It is four words and it is the refusal of Saul's armour. It does
  describe what we just watched, but it is also the thesis, and it earns the exception.
- Keep "Help me find them." — barely audible, does not name God, and is answered six seconds later
  by the river. That is the best line in the film.

### 9. [SUGGESTION] NODE 5 · 2:27 — a one-second insurance policy on the mechanism

**PROBLEM:** The physics is elegant but it has to survive a two-minute gap. The audience learns it
at 0:12–0:22 and must recall it at 2:27, across the loudest section of the film. Some viewers will
watch the sorting at 2:30 and simply not know why it is happening.

**FIX:** As the folded square lifts off his palm and is inhaled, flash-cut for four frames to the
little knot on the sand at 0:20 coming apart into five rills. Then back. It costs one second, it
makes the mechanism instantly legible to everyone, and on a second viewing it is a pleasure rather
than a crutch.

### 10. [SUGGESTION] NODE 5 · 2:12–2:20 — the fifth object's rhyme is under-sold

**PROBLEM:** "FIVE — floating past his chest, turning slowly: a mango-yellow foil wrapper." This is
a superb plant paying off across 137 seconds and the draft delivers it in one line of prose that a
viewer may not connect to 0:03.

**FIX:** When he holds it up and turns it over — foil one side, plastic the other — cut for four
frames to Kavi's hand opening at dawn, then back. Same gesture, opposite meaning: a hand opening to
throw away, a hand opening to give. The film's entire thesis in two shots that are 137 seconds
apart. That is your rewatch reward and it is currently unclaimed.

---

## WHAT'S WORKING

- **"The knot shudders. And comes apart. Not violently — it RESOLVES, the way a knot resolves when
  you find the right thread."** That verb, RESOLVES, is the whole film. Do not let anyone change it
  to a destruction word at any downstream gate.
- **The giant is SAD, not evil.** "It is not roaring. It is BREATHING." That decision is what makes
  the ending a turning of the heart instead of a victory lap, and it is what keeps the film inside
  the charter without softening the menace.
- **The auto-rickshaw.** Swallowed at 0:45 "with its indicator still blinking, patient, orange, on
  and off, all the way in," set down at 2:40 "upright, indicator still blinking, on, off, on." This
  is the single best detail in the draft. It is funny, it is precise, it carries the entire "nobody
  was harmed" promise without a line, and it is a perfect rewatch reward.
- **2:30–2:38, the sorting.** "Plastic peels away from it in a white river. Metal follows in a
  bright ribbon, the spent slugs going with it. Glass comes off in a slow glittering veil." This is
  the biggest image in the film and it is in exactly the right place — 150 seconds in, on the beat
  where the audience is most open. "It falls without a crash. It sits down." is worth more than any
  explosion and it is the reason this film is not generic.
- **Meera's gauntlets, 2:42–2:47.** She removes the armour to pick up one bottle with a bare hand.
  She takes off exactly what Arul refused to put on. Nobody says it. Perfect.
- **"He has one thing left in his hand and it is a sweet wrapper."** The whole David image in eleven
  words.
- **The theology of the fifth stone** — the object that fits none of the five bins is the one that
  works, Psalm 118:22 rendered as waste-management. Genuinely inventive weaving, never spoken aloud,
  and it survives an audience that has never read the verse.
- **The head writer's note 3** — refusing to give Arul a line explaining why it will work. That
  refusal is correct and I am reinforcing it so the room does not talk him out of it. The gap
  between what he knows and what he does is the faith, and closing it kills the film.
- **Kavi's containment.** One word of dialogue, opening careless gesture and closing careful one.
  The city's whole arc in a nine-year-old. Do not give him a second line.

---

**Summary judgement for the head writer:** this is a strong draft with a real hook and two or three
images better than most shorts get. It fails only on **distribution** — the first ten seconds are a
postcard, Node 4 is a spectacle vacuum, and the film's cleverest image (absorption of incoming fire)
is hidden behind smoke. Findings **1, 2, 4 and 5** are the ones I would fight for; **5** in
particular converts the ending montage from PSA to plot resolution at zero runtime cost.
