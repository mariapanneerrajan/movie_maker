# Seed

**Captured:** 2026-08-12

## The seed idea

*Verbatim, as Rajan gave it:*

> I want to make a movie on the essence of the story of David versus Goliath: how his faith
> overcame fear, even in the face of a lot of uncertainty, anxiety, and fear. A little boy with
> faith was able to overcome the giant. I want to tell the story in a sci-fi setting. The story
> itself, I wanted to happen in Chennai, India. There should be these huge, gigantic monsters that
> rise out of the heavily polluted Cooum river of Chennai and also from all the garbage mountains
> called Kuppa Medus. The pollution itself has been happening because of all the times people have
> been careless in just littering the place, be it the beach, roads, or parks, everywhere just
> throwing trash everywhere. They are not having strict regulations on segregating the waste,
> recycling, and disposing of the waste properly. Because of this, now we have these multiple
> giants of garbage that have risen up and are taunting the city of Chennai, destroying and walking
> on top of the Anna Flyover Bridge.
>
> Basically, we need to show all these iconic places of Chennai. I don't want to show any religious
> places. I don't want to see any Hindu temples or Muslim mosques. Just secular iconic places like
> T Nagar, Ranganathan Street, and all these different iconic, easily identifiable Chennai places
> where these monsters rise up from the Cooum River and all these Kuppa Medus. They are destroying
> different buildings and bridges and so on, and also eating up people.
>
> At that time, when everybody is afraid to fight against or to overcome these monsters, the
> protagonist Rajan comes up with a clever idea. He has faith in God, which helps him to go and do
> something quite simple and contrary to what other people might have thought. This helps him to
> make the giant fall, and then everybody gets inspired. The entire city comes together to
> segregate all the waste and dispose of the Cooum, which is completely cleaned up. Health and
> sanitation are also restored in the city. That's the seed idea for the movie that I want to make.

## What this should convey

Faith overcomes fear. Not the absence of fear — the seed is explicit that uncertainty, anxiety and
fear are all present and real. The small, unarmed, disregarded person carrying faith accomplishes
what the armed and the powerful will not attempt.

Underneath it, a second true thing: the giant is **made of what the city threw away**. The monster
is not an invader. It is the accumulated consequence of a thousand small carelessnesses, and it can
only be unmade the same way it was made — one small act at a time, by everyone.

## What the audience should feel

As the credits land: **inspired, hopeful, and implicated in a good way** — that the thing they do
tomorrow with one wrapper matters. Awe at the scale, relief at the fall, and a lift at the sight of
a whole city working together on a clean Cooum.

## Hard constraints from Rajan

- **Setting:** Chennai, India. Sci-fi.
- **Giants rise from:** the polluted Cooum river, and the *kuppa medu* garbage mountains.
- **Cause is stated in-world:** careless littering — beach, roads, parks — and the absence of
  segregation, recycling, and proper disposal.
- **Iconic Chennai locations required**, and they must be instantly identifiable: T. Nagar /
  Ranganathan Street, the Anna flyover, the Cooum, Marina Beach, and similar.
- **NO religious buildings of any kind.** No Hindu temples, no mosques, no churches. Secular
  landmarks only. *This is a hard veto and binds every prompt at every downstream gate.*
- **Rajan's solution is simple, clever, and contrary** to what everyone else assumed would work.
- **Ending:** the giant falls, the city is inspired, waste gets segregated, the Cooum is cleaned,
  health and sanitation restored.

## Charter reconciliations

Two places where the seed as given collides with `CLAUDE.md`, and how they were resolved:

1. **"Eating up people"** → charter forbids gore and dread. Resolved: the giants **swallow the
   streetscape** — buses, carts, hoardings, an auto still blinking — and people they seize are
   **carried and trapped, then coughed loose unharmed**. Menace is carried by scale, silhouette,
   and faces. Nobody is consumed, injured, or killed on screen. Fear resolves inside the film.
2. **"A little boy with faith"** → `CHR_RAJAN` is the protagonist of every film and reads late 30s.
   Resolved: *little* is rendered as **slight, unarmed, unranked, dismissed** — a conservancy worker
   the armoured response force waves aside. The David shape is intact: the one nobody sent for.
   A child may appear in the film, but does not carry it.

## Loglines

### Set 1 — 2026-08-12

1. **THE FIFTH STONE** — When a garbage giant rises from the Cooum and the city's armoured response
   force empties every weapon into it without effect, a slender conservancy worker refuses the
   exo-suit they offer him, wades into the black water for five sorted things, and aims for the one
   seam that armour was never covering.
   *Anchor:* 1 Samuel 17:38–40 — David refuses Saul's armour and chooses five smooth stones from
   the brook.

2. **WHAT THE RIVER KEPT** — Every gun in Chennai is firing at the giant and nobody has noticed
   that it is speaking; Rajan is the one man who stops, listens, and hears — in a thousand
   overlapping voices of everything the city threw away — exactly where its heart is and what it is
   asking for.
   *Anchor:* 1 Kings 19:11–12 — God was not in the wind, the earthquake, or the fire, but in the
   still small voice.

3. **THE THING THE GRID NEVER SIMULATED** — Chennai's civic defence AI runs nine million
   engagement models against the giant on the Anna flyover and every one of them fails, so an
   off-grid sanitation worker with no clearance does the one thing too small to have been modelled.
   *Anchor:* 1 Corinthians 1:27 — God chose the foolish things of the world to shame the wise, and
   the weak things to shame the strong.

4. **THE BOY ON THE FLYOVER** — Pinned on the Anna flyover with a terrified boy as the giant steps
   over them, Rajan does not tell the child it will be all right; he hands him one empty bag, tells
   him what to put in it, and the two of them start the thing that brings the giant down.
   *Anchor:* 1 Samuel 17:33 — "you are but a youth" — and Matthew 18:3, unless you become as little
   children.

5. **HEAVY LADEN** — The giant striding through T. Nagar is not attacking anyone; it is carrying
   forty years of a city's refuse and it cannot put any of it down, and Rajan is the only person in
   Chennai who looks at it long enough to understand that it is not roaring — it is asking for
   help.
   *Anchor:* Matthew 11:28 — come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden — read against
   Romans 8:22, creation groaning under what was done to it.
