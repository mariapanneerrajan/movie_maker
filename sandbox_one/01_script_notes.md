## The three round-1 vetoes and how they cleared

### 1. `scripture-guardian` — works-righteousness [BLOCKING]

The film freighted the refuse as sin and then resolved it by the city's own corporate labour — "it
can only be unmade the way it was made, by everyone," with the head writer's own note calling the
city bringing down the second giant "the whole point of the ending." Ephesians 2:8–9 inverted, and it
was the last thing the audience was told.

**Cleared three ways:** the theme line rewritten so the notes stop contradicting the film's own
chronology; the second giant no longer falls to human labour; and Meera picks up the bottle with the
bare hand Rajan showed her — imitation of a gift rather than repayment of a debt. Plus a new binding
header line: **ORDER OF OPERATIONS — rescue first, labour second; the city never earns the fall of a
giant.**

Round-2 verdict, verbatim: *"Finding #1 clears — genuinely, not by fig leaf."* The reviewer's test was
whether the film's emotional weight still credits the crowd while a technicality credits something
else. It does not: the emotional peak is 2:30–2:41, all of it before a single sack is filled, and the
crowd sequence is a reversal — they set out to do a thing and are relieved of it before they arrive.

### 2. `fact-checker` — the Cooum is sewage, not litter [BLOCKING]

The film's causal chain was litter → giant → citizens pick up litter → the river runs clear. False
about a real river: the Cooum is black from **untreated sewage** — 127 identified outfalls, ~900 more
unplugged, ~70,000 families draining illegal inlets, CPCB's most-polluted-river-in-India designation
in 2022. Citizen litter-picking does not clean it.

**Cleared and enlarged past the two-second fix asked for.** A length of concrete sewer pipe is written
into the giant's body at 0:34; the outfalls run steadily into the river at 0:30, established in the
same breath as the giant; and at 2:53 the outfall is capped with the flow going away down a new
interceptor line. The film now says the giant is made of what was thrown away **and** what was quietly
piped out of sight, and that the two are answered by different hands.

### 3. `fact-checker` — Ranganathan Street cannot hold node 3 [BLOCKING]

Carriageway ~7 m. Twenty 3 m exo-frames abreast need ~60 m. A 60 m giant has 20–25 m shoulders and
cannot enter a 7 m lane at all.

**Cleared:** node 3 restaged on Usman Road, with Ranganathan Street as the emptied slot the giant
*reaches one arm down into* — a better image than the original. Round 2 then found the line still did
not fit on Usman Road either (~17 lanes needed) and it became **four abreast and five ranks deep**, a
column in depth. Which is worse to walk a small man past than a chorus line, and it now costs him five
ranks to be walked backwards.

## Round-2 blocking items — the fact-checker correcting its own round-1 recommendation

Both were single-line repairs, and both were in material the fact-checker had itself proposed in round
1. It held its own recommendation to the standard it had applied to the Chennai Central sightline.

- **The Fort St George flagstaff is not teak and is not the tallest.** The original teak mast stood
  1688–1994 and was replaced by a steel replica; India's tallest flagpole is Attari–Wagah at 418 ft.
  Now: *"a hundred and fifty feet of it, one of the tallest in the country, where a teak mast stood for
  three hundred years."* The teak survives, attributed to time rather than to the present object.
- **"Immediately north" is 1.2 km.** Now "a kilometre north." The reviewer *did* certify the sightline
  — the mast stands on the seaward bulwark over open ground and clears the fort's low buildings, which
  is materially different from the Central tower it refused.

## The one place two reviewers wanted opposite things

**The second giant.** `entertainment-doctor` wanted it left standing and brought down **by the city**,
so the cleanup becomes plot resolution rather than a moral. `scripture-guardian` held a veto against
exactly that.

The head writer's synthesis, which the orchestrator proposed in outline and the head writer made
causally visible rather than implied: the giant **is** still standing at 2:47 and the city **does**
walk north for it with empty sacks, so the stakes are live and the audience does the arithmetic — but
the clean light Rajan released reaches it first, and what the crowd does when it arrives is **carry**.
*"Nobody is sorting anything. It is already sorted. They are gathering it."*

`entertainment-doctor` withdrew its objection in round 2: *"the synthesis is not having it both ways,
and it is better than my round-1 version… The head writer won this one."* Its reasoning: a crowd not
doing something is not a satisfying climax, but this is not the climax — the climax is the open hand
at 2:29 — and denouement is exactly where "the work turns out to be already done" belongs.

`scripture-guardian` called the light *"the strongest new thing in the draft"* — grace exceeding the
reach of the one who released it, double-readable as physics or as gift, *"which is what 'woven'
means."*

**Both reviewers then independently found the same defect in it:** the light was stated as *"faster
than anyone could follow"* and dramatised as slower than a walking crowd. They proposed the identical
fix — the light moves at exactly the speed of the crowd — which removed the contradiction and said the
theme better. **Labour walks alongside grace and never ahead of it.**

**One substitution made against the orchestrator's steer, flagged by the head writer:** the aeration
line was cut entirely and replaced with a **trash boom**. Booms are documented on these exact
waterways; aeration on the Cooum is not. A stricter fact position than either the fact-checker or the
orchestrator asked for, and it splits the ending's two causes cleanly — the capped outfall and
interceptor answer the sewage, the boom answers the litter.

## Orchestrator arbitrations

1. **The head writer's Note 3 — refusing to give Rajan any line explaining why his method will work —
   was upheld and reinforced twice.** Three reviewers independently defended it. It is now written into
   the standing production note as an instruction, not just as absent text: *"the moment Rajan can
   account for why it will work, faith becomes confidence and the story becomes an engineering
   anecdote."*
2. **`fact-checker` #9(a) chosen over #9(b)** — the pouch is empty at 2:03 because the river took what
   was there, so the fifth stone is a gift rather than an inventory item.
3. **`entertainment-doctor` #8 taken** — hyacinth on the pouch flap *and* on the returning wrapper, so
   the reading is unambiguous that the river gave it back. This closed a back-door route to the
   self-sourcing the veto was against, which the film had left undecided.
4. **The cinematographer's mode-1 findings on the boy in the netting, the intake seam, the giant's
   face, and visible safety were routed into the screenplay itself**, not left to the mode-2 pass,
   because they are charter items rather than shot craft.

## Two process failures, logged for the retrospective

**1. Review files were written to disk after the integration message that cited them.** Both rounds.
The head writer could not read the round-1 files and integrated from the orchestrator's arbitration
digest; the round-2 files were likewise still absent when it looked. No findings were lost — the digest
carried them — but the round-2 message asserted the files had been verified present when they had not
yet been written. **Lesson: write reviewer output to disk before sending the integration message that
references it.**

**2. Twelve round-1 findings were never relayed.** The orchestrator deferred the cinematographer's
mode-1 review to the mode-2 pass wholesale, and several of its findings were charter-level rather than
craft-level. The head writer found them itself when the round-1 files finally appeared during round 3,
and folded them in. **The most serious was cinematographer #3 — the boy in the netting, written as
"tangled to the waist… hands scrabbling at the mesh," which is horror grammar and had shipped
unchanged through two drafts.** It would have reached gate 4 as written. **Lesson: a reviewer's
findings must be triaged by subject, not by which agent produced them. A cinematographer can raise a
charter finding.**

## What every reviewer asked to be protected downstream

Named independently by more than one reviewer, and now written into the standing production note:

- **RESOLVES** as the verb — never a destruction word, at any gate.
- **"It falls without a crash. It sits down."**
- The giant **sad, not evil**. Not roaring. Breathing.
- The **auto-rickshaw indicator** blinking on the way in (0:45) and again on the way out (2:38).
- **Meera's two gauntlets** — she removes the armour Rajan refused to put on. Nobody says it.
- **"like a row of chairs after everyone has gone home."**
- **"he has one thing left in his hand and it is a sweet wrapper."**
- The **open hand** replacing the throw.
- **Kavi's one word**, and his arc — he is set down unharmed *before* he does anything, and only then
  becomes the one who catches the last wrapper. That ordering must not be shuffled at gate 4.
- The **question-mark pouch**: filled by Rajan (0:10), emptied by the river (2:03), refilled by the boy
  (2:57).
- The **spent casing** at 1:16 and *"he crouches, because of course he does."*
- **Frost** taking a window — the literal word, at 1:13. Not "crystalline," not "ice-like."
- *"he watches the seam open, and shut, and open, and shut, and he counts it."*
- *"a nine-year-old, who fills it without looking up"* — the clause *without looking up* is what makes
  it funny rather than sentimental.
- *"Nobody is sorting anything. It is already sorted. They are gathering it."*
- **"Every round you fire, it —" stays truncated.** The second half is never restored, at any gate.
