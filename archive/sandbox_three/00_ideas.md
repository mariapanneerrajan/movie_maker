# 00_ideas — sandbox_three

## Seed

"I want to tell the essence of the story of Jonah. How God asked him to do something, but he
disobeyed, and because of his disobedience, he was faced with a massive storm. He had to be
thrown off the ship, and the big fish swallowed him up. God did provide him safety. God rescued
his life even when Jonah disobeyed, and then, when Jonah surrendered and reached Nineveh and
obeyed God, there was a massive transformation that happened in the city of Nineveh."

What it should convey: "I want to share how sometimes our plans are not God's plans, and when we
come to a point of surrender and obey, we can make a real difference in the life of people around
us. I want to tell this story by means of using Selambatam, which is a sport in South India."

What the audience should feel: "even if they disappeared, even if they are at their lowest point,
even if they got thrown off their boat and are right now swallowed by a giant whale, there is
hope. They can surrender, and God can redeem and still accomplish the things that God has in
store for them, and they can make a big difference."

## Set 1

**1.** A fighter's spinning staff can't shield his crew from a monstrous storm; he plants it,
steps off the rail — and the sea goes glass-still as something vast and luminous rises beneath
him.
Anchor: Jonah 1:12–15 — the storm stops the instant striving stops; the deep that should kill him
is prepared to hold him.

**2.** Sealed inside a cathedral-sized dark, a man beats his staff against living walls until he
stops — and the sparks he made drift upward, lighting an immense ribbed hall rushing him toward
daylight.
Anchor: Jonah 1:17–2:9 — the fish is appointed, not sent as punishment; the confinement *is* the
rescue vehicle.

**3.** A champion walks into an enemy city's arena to settle a duel, then plants his staff upright
in the dust and opens both hands — and ten thousand raised staves come down across the city.
Anchor: Jonah 3:5–10 — the reluctant messenger reaches the city he never wanted spared, and the
whole of it turns, from the king down to the beasts.

**4.** Fleeing a summons, a man guns a hydrofoil across a dead-flat sea while vast shapes beneath
keep swinging his hull off course; he lets go of the wheel — and they lift the boat and carry it.
Anchor: Jonah 1:3–4, 1:13 — he pays the fare and goes the other way; the sea itself has been given
other instructions.

**5.** Camped above the city he resented saving, a man shelters under a vine racing up his planted
staff, watches it wither in an hour, and turns scorched and furious — as the city's lamps come on.
Anchor: Jonah 4:6–11 — the gourd, the worm, and the last question of the book: should I not pity
that great city?

**idea-scout's pick: #3** — the only one that carries both the surrender and the city-wide
transformation Rajan asked for, and the only one where Silambam is structural (an arena of raised
staves going quiet) rather than decorative. Alternate for pure awe: #1. Closest match to the exact
audience feeling described: #2.
