# LOOK — TEN THOUSAND STAVES

**Logline:** A champion walks into an enemy city's arena to settle a duel, then plants his staff
upright in the dust and opens both hands — and ten thousand raised staves come down across the city.

**Anchor:** Jonah — the reluctant messenger delivered out of the deep, arriving at the city he never
wanted spared, and the whole of it turning (Jonah 1:17–2:10 for the shore; Jonah 3:5–10 for the
arena). Carried by **the staff**: a weapon that becomes a marker the moment its owner lets go of it.
The creature that puts him ashore is the appointed deep — rescue wearing the shape of the thing that
should have ended him. *Internal only. No label, no citation, no verse appears anywhere on screen.*

**Feeling to leave behind:** The astonishment of being handed back your life, spending it on people
who wanted to strike you, and watching the whole city come down with you.

## Colour script
- **Act One (0:00–0:06):** Cold pre-dawn — near-black indigo sea, wet graphite sand, one narrow band
  of amber on the horizon and nothing else warm in frame. The creature's underlight is a dull ember
  glow through hide, never a glare.
- **Act Two (0:06–0:24):** Desaturates and narrows. Shot 2 keeps the amber horizon at his back and
  moves toward dry bone-white road and black basalt. Shots 3–4 drop the amber entirely — the sun is
  behind the west wall, so the arena is flat grey dust, chalk white, and shadow. By 0:24 the palette
  is three values of grey with one desaturated ochre.
- **Act Three (0:24–0:30):** Warmth arrives from off frame at 0:26 as the sun clears the west wall —
  low gold raking across dust, the grey bowl going honey and bronze. Shot 6 returns Act One's exact
  amber-band-over-flat-sea composition, now filling the whole frame instead of one strip.
- **Switch points for the single node's continuity block:** cold-dawn state holds through Shot 2;
  desaturated-grey state begins at **Shot 3 (0:12)** and bottoms at the end of **Shot 4 (0:24)**;
  warm state begins mid-**Shot 5**, at the sun-clears-the-wall beat, and holds through Shot 6.

## Grade and render character
Large-format digital graded toward 5219 tungsten stock pushed one stop: deep rich blacks that hold
detail, gentle highlight rolloff, fine organic grain that thickens in the shadow ranks of the crowd.
Texture over grime — salt crust, chalk, wet hide, dust in the light. No teal push, no crush, no
lens flare theatrics. One halation bloom permitted, on the sun crossing the wall in Shot 5.

## Lighting design
**Light is always something arriving from outside the frame.** Act One: a single low horizon source
behind the creature, so the leviathan and Rajan both read as silhouette against water. Act Two: the
arena's key is *blocked* — the west wall eclipses the sun, and the bowl is lit only by cold bounced
skylight, so ten thousand faces sit in shadow and nothing in frame owns its own light. Act Three:
the sun clears the wall and the key enters from screen-left, off frame, unmotivated by anything
Rajan does — mercy is not his light, it lands on him. Nobody in this film is lit as a villain; the
crowd is under-lit, never up-lit or hardened.

## Camera rule
**The camera only ever moves toward him or up away from the staff — never away from him, never
down.** Every move is single-intent: Shot 1 a slow tilt down from the creature's back to the sand;
Shot 2 a steady track with him toward the city; Shot 3 handheld-adjacent orbit through the duel;
Shot 4 a locked pull-back-in-frame achieved by the staves rising around a static camera, not by
moving; Shot 5 a push in to close; Shot 6 a rise. No compound moves.

## Lens language
- **14–24mm** — Shots 1, 2 and 6. Scale and isolation, stacked vertically: creature or city filling
  the upper two-thirds, Rajan in the lowest quarter.
- **35–50mm** — Shots 3 and 4. Everything emotional and physical lives here; the duel stays readable.
- **85–135mm** — Shot 5 only. The film's single close-up, and it must feel expensive. Tiers behind
  him compress into a wall of soft vertical lines so the lowering wave reads as pure shape change.

## Cast locks
- **RAJAN (`CHR_RAJAN`)** — in-story name **Rajan**. Late 30s, lean and wiry, 178 cm, narrow
  shoulders. Salt-and-pepper curls swept up and back, days-old stubble, rimless rectangular glasses.
  Wardrobe: a coarse undyed sailcloth wrap-trouser cut off at mid-shin, tied with a plain rope belt;
  bare chest and shoulders, no shirt; a single strip of faded indigo cloth bound around his left
  forearm. Barefoot. Skin and hair salt-crusted white at the shoulders and hairline in Acts One and
  Two; grey dust replaces the salt from Shot 3 on. Glasses stay on, water-beaded in Act One, dust-
  filmed in Act Two, catching the low gold in Act Three. No smile at any point.
- **THE CHAMPION** — head taller than Rajan, heavy-built, chalked bone-white from the waist up over
  deep brown skin, shaved head, wide leather waist-wrap dyed oxblood, brass cuffs at both wrists.
  Kept largely in silhouette and back-to-camera; he is a shape and an obstacle, never a second lead.
  His one legible close moment is Shot 5, soft in the background, laying his stave flat.

## World rules
- **Vaelur is invented** — a walled black-basalt sea-city on an unnamed coast, in no real country and
  no real period. Nothing in it may be dressed as a real historical city or a real place of worship.
- **The staff art is the governing physical culture of this world.** Movement vocabulary is drawn
  from South Indian staff fighting — wide circular sweeps, the staff spun on a low horizontal plane,
  ankle-level sweeps, both-hands-wide grip — but no real technique is named and no historical claim
  about it is made anywhere in the film. Every citizen of Vaelur carries a stave; it is dress, rank
  and argument, which is why a city laying them down is total.
- **The creature is never seen twice and never seen whole.** Only its back and the water it moves.
  It is majestic, not hunting — wonder, never dread. No teeth, no eye, no lunge, no jump-scare, and
  it makes no roar. Its one sound is water.
- **No blood anywhere.** The champion goes down hard and is unmarked. Staves strike staves, never
  bodies, except the sweep at the ankles.
- **The staves are the film's only prop.** One design, scaled by owner: shoulder-height, dark oiled
  hardwood, brass ferrule at one end, wrapped grip. Rajan's is visibly older and split at the
  butt-end — the only one in the city that has been in the sea.
- **The sea must be visible from the arena rim**, so Shot 6 can rhyme with Shot 1.
- Vertical staging law: any wide frame holds **one** human figure against **one** massive vertical
  element. Two figures only ever share a medium or closer.
