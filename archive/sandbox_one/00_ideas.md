# 00_ideas.md

## Seed

"the seed idea for the film is that I want to tell the essence of the story of David versus
Goliath, of how faith overcame fear. I want to tell this idea in the context of a sci-fi setting
happening in the future on planet Earth. I want the battle to be between humans living in a
utopian city vs monsters and strange creatures that are trying to take over the city. By watching
this short movie, the audience should feel a sense of confidence that they can overcome any odds
if they believe and have faith."

## Set 1

1. A colossus grows on the city's fear; one unarmed engineer walks steadily toward it without
   flinching, until the giant starves down to a trembling creature small enough to hold in his palm.
   Anchor: 1 Samuel 17 — the giant's real weapon is forty days of taunting that paralyses an
   entire army before a blow is struck; David's question reframes the size of the thing. Paired
   with the pattern that perfect love casts out fear.
   Why it works: the monster visibly inflates off every flinching face in the plaza and deflates
   with every unhurried step Rajan takes — the turn is that the terror was the only thing that
   was ever big.

2. Sealed into the city's last champion-mech and unable to take a single step in it, a dock-rigger
   climbs out, whirls the cargo tether he has swung for twenty years, and drops the siege-beast.
   Anchor: 1 Samuel 17:38-40 — "I cannot go in these, for I have not proved them"; David lays
   down the king's armour and takes the shepherd's sling, the tool of his own small history.
   Why it works: the hatch hissing open on a gleaming war-machine and a man in work clothes
   stepping out of it is the whole thesis in one image; the turn is refusal, not firepower.

3. As a spire-city flees upward from creatures pouring out of the flooded dark below, one man rides
   the last lift down alone to relight the root-lamp — and light climbs back up behind him.
   Anchor: David alone descends into the valley of Elah while both armies hold the safe high
   ground; joined to the pattern of a light that shines in the darkness and is not overcome.
   Why it works: a vertical frame built for 9:16 — every other body moving up, one moving down —
   and the turn is the glow overtaking him from beneath and racing the creatures off the tower.

4. A colossal creature's roar freezes a utopian city in visible rings of pressure; a lone bell-tuner
   walks into the plaza and answers with one small clear note that shatters the giant's throat.
   Anchor: 1 Samuel 17:8-11 — the defiance is a voice, repeated morning and evening, and Israel
   is "dismayed and greatly afraid" by sound alone until one voice answers it.
   Why it works: sound made fully visible — vast shuddering shockwave rings versus one perfect
   thin ring of light that passes through them all and keeps going.

5. After the war-fleet falls, a drone-keeper's ragged little flock lifts him to the siege-beast's one
   bare seam, where he plants a seed of light and the monster blooms open into a grove.
   Anchor: the shepherd credentialed by the lion and the bear — years of unwitnessed care over
   small creatures turn out to be the training for the giant; the least is made greatest.
   Why it works: battered maintenance drones he has patched for years carrying him up a mountain
   of armour, and the turn is a weapon that ends the siege by making something grow.

**idea-scout's recommendation: #1** — the seed's own line, "faith overcame fear," is the literal
physics of the world, so the transfer survives a rewatch: the viewer watches the thing that
terrified a city shrink because one calm man would not flinch, closing on a once-vast creature
trembling in a cupped palm.
