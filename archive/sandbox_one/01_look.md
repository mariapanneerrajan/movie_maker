# LOOK — THE SHRINKING GIANT

**Logline:** A colossus grows on the city's fear; one unarmed engineer walks toward it while
afraid, and when he falls the city rises with him, until the giant starves down to a trembling
creature small enough to hold in his palm.
**Anchor:** *(internal only — never on screen, never in a prompt)* The shepherd and the champion:
the giant's real weapon is forty days of taunting that paralyses an army before a blow is struck,
and one person walking out resizes him for everyone watching — after which the paralysed army
rises and follows. Paired with the pattern that perfect love casts out fear. **The symbol is the
fear-thread** — fear made visible as ice-blue filament, the giant's only food, which turns gold
and breaks the moment a person faces it. No verse, no name, no label anywhere downstream.
**Feeling to leave behind:** He was terrified the whole way — and it still shrank. So can mine,
and I will not be standing there alone.

## Colour script
- **Act One (0:00–0:06):** a living city at dusk. Warm white glass and pale stone, deep indigo sky,
  one hot amber band low on the horizon, and **green everywhere** — terraces, hanging vines, trees
  standing on rooftops, water running through planted channels.
- **The saturation rule that keeps green and blue from fighting — state it in every downstream
  prompt:** *the fear-thread is the only **emissive** colour in the film; the greenery is the only
  **reflective** colour.* Foliage at dusk sits low in the luminance range — deep viridian, moss and
  sage in shadow, never vivid emerald, never lit from within — while the threads are self-luminous
  ice-blue filaments that read graphically against it. They never compete, because they never
  occupy the same tonal register. Greenery holds the lower third and the mid-ground terraces;
  threads own the vertical air above them.
- **Everything worn in this film sits outside both registers.** No blue and no gold on any garment,
  and no green on any garment — cloth is plant-dyed, low-chroma and warm-neutral (flax, oatmeal,
  clay brown, walnut, warm charcoal), so the only blue in frame is fear and the only gold is grace.
  All metal on any person is blackened, oxidised or matte, never bright, never chrome.
- **Act Two (0:06–0:24):** the amber band is gone from the first frame of shot 2. **The green
  drains — this is now a story instrument, not a grade note.** Under the colossus's shadow the
  foliage goes ash-grey and blue, terrace by terrace, at the same rate the threads feed it, so the
  city visibly loses its life as the giant gains it. Whites go blue-grey, contrast climbs, shadows
  deepen. **One warm exception, and it is the whole midpoint:** at 0:15–0:17 a single gold thread
  burns on Rajan's chest — the only warmth in the act — extinguished at 0:21 so its loss is felt.
  By 0:24 the frame is the coldest and deadest in the film: near-monochrome blue-black, grey
  leaves, chalk highlights, and one blazing cold core — the open throat of the colossus at 0:21,
  the brightest object in the film and the only time its mouth is a light source.
- **Act Three (0:24–0:30):** gold floods in at the first frame of shot 4, raking low across wet
  stone, then multiplying outward as the crowd's threads turn — the warmth is *caused* by the
  people. Blue drains avenue by avenue and **the green comes back with the gold**, colour running
  back up the terraces in the same wave. Shot 5 returns Act One's exact palette — white glass,
  amber, green — but amber is now the key, the green is a soft out-of-focus field behind his hand,
  and the scale is a palm, not a skyline.
- **The single node's continuity block states all three states and their switch shots:** palette A
  at shot 1, palette B switching at shot 2 (with the gold-thread exception inside shot 3), palette
  C switching at shot 4. Never a film-wide average palette.

## Grade and render character
**Full photoreal, large-format blockbuster capture** — the register of a modern creature tentpole,
not an illustration. Filmic highlight rolloff, deep true blacks that stay faintly blue, fine
organic grain, shallow falloff on the long lenses, real atmosphere between camera and subject:
dusk haze, drifting spray off the planted channels, dust lifting on the shockwave. The colossus is
rendered with full physical weight — subsurface glow inside the porcelain, micro-craze and soot in
every seam, visible mass and inertia, ground shake, air displaced by the tail. It should look
photographed, and it should look enormous.

**The safety line is held by surface and staging, not by underplaying the render.** Matte glaze
throughout: no wet gloss on teeth, eyes or jaw, no saliva, no drool, no gums or tongue detail, no
scarring, no viscera, no blood, no gore, no wound, no injury to any living thing anywhere in the
film. Nothing is staged as a jump scare — no sudden lunge into lens, no reveal cut, no held silence
broken by a shock. The jaw opens exactly once, aimed at the sky, and **no shot ever frames the open
jaw and a human being in the same composition.** Dread is not the currency here; awe is. The
colossus should make a viewer's stomach drop the way scale and weather do, never the way a
predator closing on a child does.

## Lighting design
**Through Act Two the colossus is the only light in the world** — a cold source that brightens as
it eats, glowing out of the seams between its plates, so the city is lit by its own dread. Rajan is
edge-lit blue from above and unlit from the front; his face is hard to read and that is the point.
At 0:21 the jaw opens and the throat becomes a second, far harsher source — a cold blue furnace
firing up into the cloud and washing back down across the plaza. Hardest light in the film, about
a second long.
**In Act Three the light arrives from a source he did not make and cannot control:** gold from
behind the shutter that closed on him at 0:06, entering below the frame line, reaching his hand
before his face — then spreading as hundreds of gold threads ignite across the plaza and up the
terraces. The small work lamp on his harness is the one light he made himself; it illuminates
nothing all film until the final shot, where it is the only light on the creature. Warmth is grace
arriving. Cold is absence — a frightened crowd is never lit as a fault, and the colossus is lit as
a force of weather, not as an evil thing.

## Camera rule
**The camera only ever moves toward Rajan.** One move per shot, every move ending closer to him
than it began — shot 1's crane falls out of the sky to find him; shots 2 and 3 track and push;
shot 4 pushes past the crowd's silhouettes to him. Shot 5 does not move: the film has arrived.

## Lens language
Wide (16–21mm) carries scale and isolation — shot 1's crane, and the surge inside shot 3 — with the
frame stacked vertically: planted foreground low, Rajan filling the middle height, the colossus
filling all the headroom. Normal (35–50mm) carries the walk and the buckle in shot 2, and the crowd
streaming past in shot 4. Long (85–100mm) carries the turns that matter: his eyes lifting at 0:15,
his face on the stone at 0:23, the cupped hand in shot 5. The close-up is scarce on purpose.

## Cast and environment locks
- **RAJAN (`CHR_RAJAN`)** — in-story name **Rajan**. Late 30s, 5'10", lean and slender, narrow
  shoulders, no bulk. Medium-deep South Asian brown skin, long oval face, deep-set steady dark
  eyes, days-old grey-flecked stubble. Dense curly salt-and-pepper hair swept up and back, grey
  heaviest at temples and front hairline. Thin fully rimless rectangular glasses, faint blue-violet
  lens sheen — **on in every frame except 0:22–0:25, where they are on the paving.**
  - **Wardrobe** — the same plant-dyed cloth family as the crowd, separated by *value*, not by
    hue: he is the lightest figure in a field of mid-tones, which is what makes him read the
    instant he stands up. **Undyed raw-flax canvas work coverall in faded oatmeal**, softened and
    thinned by washing, faint sheen where it has worn at the knee and elbow, doubled clay-brown
    patches at knees and elbows, concealed front placket with no visible hardware, sleeves rolled
    to the elbow. **Oiled walnut-brown leather-and-webbing tool harness** across the chest — matte,
    dark, warm — carrying a coil of unbleached hemp-jacketed cable in warm sand, blackened-steel
    magnetic clips, a short blackened spanner in a loop, and folded work gloves at the hip. **One
    small brass-bodied work lamp at the sternum, clipped just left of centre** — dull oxidised
    bronze, unlit and never catching a highlight until 0:28. Oiled clay-brown leather boots, matte,
    soles worn. **Nothing on him is blue, gold or green, and no metal on him is bright** — the only
    blue in his frame is fear and the only gold is grace.
  - **Harness rule, load-bearing:** the straps are cut to leave the centre of his chest, sternum to
    collar, as clear pale cloth. **The fear-thread lifts from that clear field in every shot** and
    is never crossed, hidden or upstaged by webbing, buckle or lamp. Pale oatmeal is chosen
    precisely so an ice-blue filament, and later a gold one, reads hard against it.
  - **Unarmed in every frame.** Performance lock: **he is visibly afraid from shot 1 to the last
    frame** — shaking hands, shallow breath, a buckling knee, a face that comes apart at 0:23, a
    hand still trembling at 0:29. Never heroic-calm, never stoic, never smiling. What reads is
    effort, not fearlessness.

- **THE COLOSSUS (`CHR_COLOSSUS`)** — **a photoreal dinosaur-dragon, two hundred metres at first
  sight, and still growing.**
  - **Build:** theropod frame. Enormous digitigrade hind legs, deep barrel chest, spine pitched
    forward, a long heavy counterweight tail that drags across avenues. Forelimbs shorter but
    massive, three blunt claws per hand, knuckles set down on rooftops so it *leans on the city*.
    It stoops. It never stands politely upright.
  - **Dragon half:** a spined dorsal ridge of tall porcelain blades running crest to tail-tip; two
    long ridged horns sweeping back off the skull; ragged half-furled wing-spars high on the
    shoulders — too tattered to fly, wide enough to put a district in shadow.
  - **Head:** long and low-slung on a thick neck, thrust forward and down toward street level. Deep
    crocodilian jaw, blunt-tipped snout, heavy overhanging brow. **A real mouth that opens wide** —
    two ranks of large teeth, bone-white, conical and blunt-tipped rather than needle-fine, uneven
    the way old teeth are, dry and matte. Beyond them the throat is a shaft of blue furnace-light.
    **The jaw opens exactly once in the film, at 0:21, and it roars at the sky.**
  - **Eyes:** narrow ice-blue slits sunk deep under the brow ridge, pupil-less, lit from within.
    Fixed and pitiless the way weather is pitiless. Not round, not wide, not curious.
  - **Surface — load-bearing for the story, keep it exactly:** chalk-white porcelain plates lapped
    like armour scale and osteoderm, craze-cracked, soot-darkened in the seams, with ice-blue smoke
    churning visibly in the gaps and behind the ribs. Seams brighten as it feeds; **the glaze goes
    dull in grey patches as it starves, and that is how the audience sees it dying.**
  - **Presence:** it is tormenting the city — head lowered into the streets, tail sweeping the
    avenues, claws on the towers, wing-spars blacking out the sky, glass shivering as it moves.
    Formidable, dangerous, spectacular. It never needs to touch anyone to be terrifying, and it
    never does.
  - **Scale lock:** **its silhouette is identical at every size.** In shot 5 it is palm-sized — the
    same long jaw, shut; teeth like grass seed; horns like fish bones; wing-spars folded down; the
    glaze dull and hairline-cracked; the eyes two dim sparks with no fire behind them; the tail
    curled around its own feet. Trembling. A fallen dragon the size of a sparrow.

- **THE WHITE CITY (environment)** — a **thriving, open, luscious** utopia at dusk. Named for its
  pale glass and stone, never for an absence of life.
  - **Open, not colonnaded.** No stone columns, no cement, no ceremonial arcade, no enclosure. The
    central plaza is a wide unroofed civic clearing that sightlines run straight through.
  - **Glass first:** slender curved towers in warm low-iron glass, floor-to-sky curtain walls,
    glass footbridges spanning between towers, glass canopies on hairline steel over the plaza
    edges, terraced glass balconies stepping back as the towers rise. Light passes through this
    city rather than bouncing off it.
  - **Green everywhere:** planted terraces spilling vines down whole facades, mature trees standing
    on rooftops and in the plaza itself, moss beds and ferns between the paving, tall grasses at
    the water's edge, hanging gardens under every footbridge. The city looks cultivated and loved.
  - **Water:** shallow planted channels and reflecting shelves running through the plaza; the
    paving is wet, which is what carries the gold light at 0:24.
  - **Ground plane:** pale stone and glass paving, seamless, unbroken by kerbs.
  - **The shelter:** a broad glass transit pavilion at the plaza's edge with a heavy steel blast
    shutter that drops from a recess in its lintel to seal it. Mechanism unchanged from the script
    — it DROPS at 0:06 and GRINDS UP at 0:24.
  - No signage, lettering, writing, logos, flags or numerals on any surface, in any language.

- **THE CROWD** — silhouette or near-silhouette only, never a featured face, never a named
  individual. Low-chroma plant-dyed everyday cloth — flax, oatmeal, clay brown, walnut, warm
  charcoal — pitched a step or two darker in value than Rajan's coverall, so he separates from them
  without wearing a different colour. One anonymous open hand enters frame at 0:26. No second
  character has an arc.

## World rules
- Fear is visible: an ice-blue thread lifting from a person's chest and rising. **Everyone emits
  one, Rajan included, all film.** Being afraid is the normal human state here, not a failing.
- A thread turns **gold and breaks** the moment its owner faces the giant instead of hiding from
  it. Gold cannot be eaten. This is the only rule the film teaches, taught by image alone.
- The colossus grows where blue threads land and shrinks the instant they stop. **Nothing physical
  affects it** — no weapon appears anywhere in the film.
- **The teeth are for roaring, never for biting.** It never bites, snaps, lunges at, swipes at,
  grabs, chews or touches a human being at any point. Its jaw opens once, at 0:21, aimed up into
  the cloud, and what comes out is sound and light. The only force it exerts on anyone is the
  shockwave of that roar, which knocks Rajan off his feet and injures no one.
- **No person or animal is shown harmed anywhere in the film,** and no building is destroyed. The
  city is not wrecked, only drained — the damage the colossus does is to colour and to nerve.
- The city is invented. No real place, institution, technology or measurement of the actual world.
- No dialogue, voiceover, narration, or on-screen text in any frame. Named sound cues only: the
  shutter's iron note at 0:06, the colossus's ROAR at 0:21 — a deep dinosaur bellow with a cracked
  glass edge, not a wet animal snarl.
